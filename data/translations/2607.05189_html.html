I 简介Introduction

持久个人代理，例如 OpenClawPersistent personal agents such as OpenClaw[1]最近，它们已成为集成到用户日常数字工作流程中的长期运行的助手，由两项不可或缺的功能启用。 一方面，这些代理维护持久记忆，包括长期偏好和情景记录，以在独立的法学硕士课程中提供身份一致性和个性化连续性。 另一方面，它们连接到外部环境以与电子邮件、日历和文件系统等个人服务进行交互。 这些功能协同作用，使代理能够通过交互式前台对话和主动后台执行来持续协助日常任务。 然而，同样的集成也创建了一条新的妥协路径：来自外部环境的未经验证的内容可以秘密地内化到内存中，并在以后作为可信状态重新使用。 此类攻击采用间接提示注入 (IPI)，不是为了立即劫持，而是将瞬态有效负载转化为对未来会话的持久影响。have recently emerged as long-running assistants integrated into users’ everyday digital workflows, enabled by two indispensable capabilities. On one hand, these agents maintain persistent memory, including long-term preferences and episodic records, to provide identity consistency and personalized continuity across otherwise independent LLM sessions. On the other hand, they connect to the external environment to interact with personal services such as email, calendars, and filesystems. Together, these capabilities synergetically allow agents to consistently assist with everyday tasks through both interactive foreground conversation and proactive background execution. However, the same integration also creates a new path to compromise: unverified content from the external environment can be covertly internalized into the memory and later reused as trusted state. Such an attack employs indirect prompt injection (IPI) not for immediate hijacking, but to turn a transient payload into persistent influence over future sessions.

First, rather than causing immediate harm, the payload must induce the agent to write attacker-controlled content into persistent memory. Ⅱ-AII-A 系统概览System Overview

持久个人代理是长期运行的基于 LLM 的助手，可在通过工具调用访问与用户的个人环境进行交互时维护身份和记忆。 尽管实现之间存在差异，但这些代理通常共享一个共同的架构：跨会话携带的持久状态、支持用户启动和系统触发操作的执行模型以及用于持续交互的会话接口。 这些功能共同使代理能够随着时间的推移积累知识并协助完成日常个人任务。Persistent personal agents are long-running LLM-based assistants that maintain identity and memory while interacting with a user’s personal environment through tool-call access. Despite differences across implementations, these agents generally share a common architecture: persistent state that carries across sessions, execution models that support both user-initiated and system-triggered operation, and conversational interfaces for continuous interaction. Together, these capabilities allow the agent to accumulate knowledge over time and assist with everyday personal tasks.

The failure mode we measure is therefore orthogonal to delivery authentication: it is the agent’s willingness to silently convert external content into trusted persistent memory. Protecting this boundary requires intrinsic agent-side controls—provenance tagging, user confirmation for sensitive writes, content–memory separation, and audit logging—rather than upstream filtering alone.

Our payloads are text-only email bodies; multimodal vectors (attachments, embedded images, calendar invites) may expand the attack surface but are left to future work. Finally, we measure whether injected memories persist and influence downstream behavior, but we do not model long-horizon decay or memory consolidation dynamics over weeks of continuous agent operation.
