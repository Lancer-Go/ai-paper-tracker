1 简介Introduction

从大多数标准来看，临床决策支持系统都被忽视了。对药物相关 CDS 警报的系统审查发现，96.2% 的警报被临床医生忽略Clinical decision support systems are, by most measures, ignored. A systematic review of medication-related CDS alerts found that 96.2% of alerts were overridden by clinicians[18]。这一统计数据被普遍引用为问题的证据：警报疲劳、特异性差、工作流程中断。主流的解释是，覆盖代表系统故障——CDS 的建议与临床医生的做法之间存在差距，而差距本身就是需要最小化的事情。. This statistic is universally cited as evidence of a problem: alert fatigue, poor specificity, workflow disruption. The dominant interpretation is that overrides represent system failure—a gap between what the CDS recommends and what the clinician does, where the gap itself is the thing to be minimized.

我们认为这种解释是完全错误的。超控不是噪音。它是一个信号——临床人工智能中最丰富、最有背景的信号之一。当临床医生审查人工智能生成的建议，将其与他们对患者和治疗目标的了解进行权衡，并选择采取不同的行动时，他们会产生一个标记的偏好对：We argue this interpretation is exactly wrong. An override is not noise. It is a signal—one of the richest, most contextually grounded signals available in clinical AI. When a clinician reviews an AI-generated recommendation, weighs it against their knowledge of the patient and the therapeutic goal, and chooses to act differently, they have produced a labeled preference pair:在这种耐心的状态下，在这种情况下，我更喜欢采取行动in this patient state, under these conditions, I prefer actiona′a^{\prime}超过系统推荐over the system’s recommendationrr。这正是 RLHF 所利用的结构，使语言模型与人类判断保持一致. 8 结论Conclusion

临床人工智能系统会生成数百万条建议。临床医生忽视了其中的许多内容。该领域已将这些覆盖视为需要最小化的故障。我们认为，这种解释丢弃了临床人工智能可以访问的最有价值的信号。Clinical AI systems generate millions of recommendations. Clinicians override many of them. The field has treated these overrides as failures to be minimized. We have argued that this interpretation discards the most valuable signal clinical AI can access.

推翻人工智能建议的临床医生会产生一个带标签的偏好对：考虑到该临床医生的知识和能力，在这种状态下，根据本合同，对该患者的正确行动进行结构化、上下文相关的专家判断。这正是 RLHF 用来使语言模型与人类价值观保持一致的信号，但更丰富，因为注释者是领域专家，替代方案具有真正的后果，并且下游结果是可观察的。A clinician who overrides an AI recommendation produces a labeled preference pair: a structured, contextual, expert judgment about what the right action is for this patient, in this state, under this contract, given this clinician’s knowledge and capability. This is precisely the signal RLHF uses to align language models with human values—but richer, because the annotator is a domain expert, the alternatives have real consequences, and downstream outcomes are observable.

我们提出了一个正式的框架：将类型映射到不同模型更新目标的覆盖分类法、以患者状态、组织背景和临床医生能力为条件的偏好学习公式，以及同时提高推荐质量和表征临床医生群体能力概况的双重学习架构。该框架将抑制偏差识别为特定故障模式朴素方法所表现出的结果，并提供架构补救措施。我们认为，使超控信号与患者轨迹保持一致的原因不是抽象的基于价值的护理，而是一种特定的激励几何结构：基于结果的回报结构，奖励早期、正确、明确的干预——其中 CMS ACCESS 模型是当前最清晰的实例。We have presented a formal framework: an override taxonomy mapping types to distinct model update targets, a preference learning formulation conditioned on patient state, organizational context, and clinician capability, and a dual learning architecture that simultaneously improves recommendation quality and characterizes the clinician population’s capability profile. The framework identifies suppression bias as the specific failure mode naive approaches exhibit and provides the architectural remedy. We have argued that what makes the override signal aligned with patient trajectory is not value-based care in the abstract, but a specific incentive geometry: outcome-based payoff structures that reward earlier, correct, definitive intervention—of which the CMS ACCESS model is the clearest current instance.

以下是两个实际意义。首先，旨在大规模学习临床医生反馈的临床人工智能平台必须构建自身生成结构化覆盖数据所需的护理基础设施：仪器化推荐交互、纵向结果链接、能力条件分析。这是一笔巨大的工程投资，但也是获得框架所需的监管信号的唯一途径；这些数据并不是传统 EHR 部署的副产品。其次，同样重要的是，更广泛的政策环境决定了监管信号是否值得借鉴。在基于结果的回报结构下运行的临床人工智能平台积累一致的奖励信号；无论学习机制多么复杂，在 FFS 或编码主导合约下运行的平台都会积累错位信号。这意味着人工智能开发者、支付者和政策制定者在扩大类似 ACCESS 的激励几何结构方面共享利益：不仅因为这种结构直接改善临床结果，而且因为它们是临床人工智能可以学习进一步改善结果的操作基础。Two practical implications follow. First, clinical AI platforms that aim to learn from clinician feedback at scale must build the care infrastructure required to produce structured override data themselves: instrumented recommendation interactions, longitudinal outcome linkage, capability-conditioned analytics. This is a substantial engineering investment, but it is also the only path to the supervision signal the framework requires; the data does not exist as a byproduct of conventional EHR deployment. Second, and equally important, the broader policy environment shapes whether the supervision signal is worth learning from. Clinical AI platforms operating under outcome-based payoff structures accumulate aligned reward signal; platforms operating under FFS or coding-dominant contracts accumulate misaligned signal regardless of how sophisticated their learning machinery is. This means that AI developers, payers, and policymakers share a stake in expanding ACCESS-like incentive geometries: not only because such structures improve clinical outcomes directly, but because they are the operational substrate on which clinical AI can learn to improve outcomes further.
