摘要Abstract

人工智能正在改变科学研究——不仅仅是作为一种更 强大的工具，但作为研究周期本身的自主参与者。 从最精确的意义上来说，这种转变构成了 研究产业化：从工艺模式的转变，其中知识、方法、 和判断被嵌入到研究人员的管道模型中，其中这些步骤 被分解、自动化和监督。美国能源部的起源 使命是当前这一转变最雄心勃勃的实例，但根本性的 它提出的问题远远超出任何单一计划。本文探讨七 这些问题：科学的代际传播受到侵蚀 能力；人工智能生成的理论越来越不透明；同行的崩溃 在大量机器生成的输出下进行评估；人工智能未经证实的能力 范式转变的发现；政治和经济领域对科学议程的掌控 工业参与者；闭环管道中系统误差的复合；和 全球研究界的结构性分歧，形成不可通约的层级。 这些担忧并不构成反对人工智能驱动科学的论据——人工智能驱动的科学 所展示的潜力是真实且重要的。它们构成了以下条件 可以负责任地发挥这种潜力。Artificial intelligence is transforming scientific research — not merely as a more powerful instrument, but as an autonomous participant in the research cycle itself. This transition constitutes, in the most precise sense of the term, the industrialization of research: a shift from a craft model, in which knowledge, method, and judgment are embedded in the researcher, to a pipeline model, in which these steps are decomposed, automated, and supervised. The US Department of Energy’s Genesis Mission is the most ambitious current instantiation of this shift, but the fundamental questions it raises extend far beyond any single program. This essay examines seven such questions: the erosion of the intergenerational transmission of scientific competence; the growing opacity of AI-generated theories; the collapse of peer evaluation under a flood of machine-generated output; the unproven capacity of AI for paradigm-shifting discovery; the capture of the scientific agenda by political and industrial actors; the compounding of systematic errors in closed-loop pipelines; and the structural bifurcation of the global research community into incommensurable tiers. These concerns do not constitute an argument against AI-driven science — whose demonstrated potential is real and significant. They constitute the conditions under which that potential can be responsibly pursued.

2. 为什么是人工智能驱动的科学？2. Why AI-Driven Science?

8. 科学的结构分歧8. The Structural Bifurcation of Science

不平等获取人工智能基础设施的标准框架是一门“多速科学”——一些机构正在采取行动 有些人更快，有些人慢一些，但都朝着同一个方向。大规模人工智能科学计划可能会产生什么 不是速度差距，而是组织分歧：科学实践的两种形式逐渐变得越来越少 可通约，失去了使他们之间能够进行批判性评价的共同语言。The standard framing of unequal access to AI infrastructure is a “multi-speed science” — some institutions move faster, others slower, but all in the same direction. What large-scale AI science initiatives are likely to produce is not a speed gap but an organizational bifurcation: two forms of scientific practice that become progressively less commensurable, losing the shared language that makes critical evaluation possible between them.

这种不对称性同时在两个方向上发挥作用，其中一个方向几乎没有受到关注。研究人员 外部先进的人工智能基础设施将继续发布——他们的结果将立即被 他们无法访问的系统。大型人工智能科学平台旨在吸收已发表的整个语料库 科学。此类系统之外的研究人员会自动为系统的生产力做出贡献，而这些系统在任何方面都无法提供任何帮助。 返回。他们的论文被吸收，他们的数据集被吸收，他们的发现被用来训练下一代模型 ——尽管他们仍然无法访问、复制甚至完全阅读这些模型所产生的科学成果。这是 一种没有真正先例的认知提取形式：以前的资源不对称让更富裕的机构更好 工具，但它们并不涉及从资源较少的机构系统地收集智力成果 为资源更丰富的企业提供动力。The asymmetry operates in both directions at once, and one direction has received almost no attention. Researchers outside advanced AI infrastructure will continue to publish — and their results will be immediately ingested by the systems they cannot access. Large-scale AI science platforms are designed to absorb the entire corpus of published science. Researchers outside such systems contribute automatically to the productivity of systems that offer nothing in return. Their papers are ingested, their datasets absorbed, their findings used to train the next generation of models — while they remain unable to access, replicate, or even fully read the science those models produce in return. This is a form of epistemic extraction with no real precedent: previous resource asymmetries gave richer institutions better tools, but they did not involve the systematic harvesting of intellectual output from less-resourced institutions to power the engines of more-resourced ones.

下游后果加剧了本文前面提出的所有担忧。当同行评审失去基础时 作者和审稿人不再在相同的认知环境中工作。人才流失加速流向少数人 拥有先进人工智能基础设施的机构——正是人类研究人员最有可能被取代的地方。 当科学的职业逻辑——积累专业知识、发表文章、为累积对话做出贡献——就会崩溃 对话的一方的运作速度和规模是另一方无法跟上的。The downstream consequences compound every concern raised earlier in this essay. Peer review loses its foundation when authors and reviewers no longer work in the same epistemic environment. Brain drain accelerates toward the handful of institutions with advanced AI infrastructure — precisely where the human researcher is most at risk of being replaced. And the career logic of science — build expertise, publish, contribute to a cumulative conversation — breaks down when one side of the conversation operates at a speed and scale the other cannot follow.

出现的不是科学的全球加速，而是科学的分裂成两个基本上互不交流的国家 做法：一是快速、不透明、制度集中；另一个则以人类为尺度且日益边缘化。 危险不是第二个消失，而是它失去了批判性地参与、挑战或 纠正第一个——随之而来的是，科学失去了分布式怀疑论，而分布式怀疑论是科学对抗科学的唯一可靠防御手段。 自己的错误。What emerges is not a global acceleration of science but its fragmentation into two largely non-communicating practices: one fast, opaque, and institutionally concentrated; the other human-scaled and increasingly marginalized. The danger is not that the second disappears, but that it loses the ability to critically engage with, challenge, or correct the first — and with it, science loses the distributed skepticism that is its only reliable defense against its own errors.

9. 评估的崩溃9. The Collapse of Evaluation

接下来的担忧更加制度化，但同样严重。科学界规范质量 它的输出通过同行评审、编辑判断、复制和集体批评的系统进行。这个系统是 不完美——它很慢，容易出现偏差，并且从未完全解决可重复性的问题。但这取决于一个 关键假设：有足够多合格的人类专家来阅读、评估和挑战正在开展的工作 产生的。The concern that follows is more institutional, but no less serious. The scientific community regulates the quality of its output through a system of peer review, editorial judgment, replication, and collective criticism. This system is imperfect — it is slow, prone to bias, and has never fully solved the problem of reproducibility. But it rests on a crucial assumption: that there are enough qualified human experts to read, evaluate, and challenge the work being produced.

如果人工智能系统大规模生成科学论文——这不是一个推测的场景，它已经发生了—— 这个假设不成立。没有一个人类审稿人群体能够评估当前出版物数量的十倍。 答案可能是使用人工智能来审查人工智能生成的论文，这简单地解决了问题：谁来评估 审稿人？我们最终可能会得到一个系统，其中论文由人工智能生成，由人工智能审核，由人工智能接受或拒绝 AI，并被 AI 引用——一个闭环，在定量意义上是富有成效的，但与任何事物越来越脱节。 人类科学判断的真正共同体。If AI systems generate scientific papers at scale — and this is not a speculative scenario, it is already happening — this assumption collapses. No community of human reviewers can evaluate ten times the current volume of publications. The response might be to use AI to review AI-generated papers, which simply displaces the problem: who evaluates the reviewers? We could end up with a system in which papers are generated by AI, reviewed by AI, accepted or rejected by AI, and cited by AI — a closed loop that is productive in a quantitative sense but increasingly disconnected from any real community of human scientific judgment.

历史在这里并没有提供多少安慰。科学的发表总是超出其应有的数量，而且出版物的数量 一个多世纪以来不断增长。没有任何机制可以使产出激增自然地导致 质量相应提高。更有可能的结果是更多的噪音——更多的增量结果，更多的论文 这无法增进任何人的理解，更难以确定真正重要的工作。History offers little comfort here. Science has always published more than it should, and the volume of publications has grown continuously for over a century. There is no mechanism by which a surge in output naturally leads to a corresponding improvement in quality. The more likely outcome is more noise — more incremental results, more papers that advance no one’s understanding, more difficulty identifying the work that actually matters.

一个相关的挑战涉及研究人员自身的评估。学术生涯依赖于定量 信号（出版物数量、引用指数）和定性判断（阅读作品、采访候选人、 评估研究人员是否能够解释和捍卫他们的想法）。人工智能驱动的科学破坏了两者的稳定：五十 人工智能辅助论文一年不会告诉你任何关于科学质量的信息，“告诉我你是如何得出这个结论的” 当结果是由研究人员的代理人产生时，“结果”确实变得更难回答和评估 监督而不是派生自己。人类和机器贡献之间的界限变得模糊 善意的小组将很难驾驭——这不仅适用于宣传，也适用于出版。A related challenge concerns the evaluation of researchers themselves. Academic careers rest on both quantitative signals (publication counts, citation indices) and qualitative judgment (reading the work, interviewing the candidate, assessing whether the researcher can explain and defend their ideas). AI-driven science destabilizes both: fifty AI-assisted papers a year tells you nothing about scientific quality, and “walk me through how you arrived at this result” becomes truly harder to answer — and to evaluate — when the result was produced by an agent the researcher supervised rather than derived themselves. The boundary between human and machine contribution is blurred in ways that good-faith panels will struggle to navigate — and this applies to promotion as much as to publication.

招聘提出了一个独特但相关的问题，而且是双向的。擅长指导的研究员 如果没有该基础设施，或者在五年内使用不同版本的基础设施，大规模人工智能工作流程可能会丢失。的 反之亦然。这给早期职业招聘带来了一个特殊的挑战：我们如何评估博士生或 博士后的科学能力是在人工智能驱动的研究环境中形成的？相反，我们如何评估 传统培训的候选人在他们从未工作过的世界中的潜力是什么？我们还没有这些问题的答案 问题——今天招聘委员会做出的决定将塑造未来的科学劳动力 三十年。Hiring raises a distinct but related problem, and it cuts in both directions. A researcher who excels at directing large-scale AI workflows may be lost without that infrastructure — or with a different version of it in five years. The reverse is equally true. This creates a specific challenge for early-career hiring: how do we evaluate a PhD student or postdoc whose scientific ability was formed in an AI-driven research environment? And conversely, how do we assess a traditionally trained candidate’s potential in a world they have never worked in? We do not yet have answers to these questions — and the decisions being made in hiring committees today will shape the scientific workforce for the next thirty years.

这并不意味着系统会简单地崩溃。科学家和机构将会适应。但我们应该诚实地说 挑战的规模。评估研究和研究人员的整个工具——指标、期刊、资助 审查、推广小组——是为一个科学成果由人类以人类速度产生的世界而设计的 头脑。那个世界正在终结。取代它的东西需要经过深思熟虑的设计，而不是在压力下临时凑合。This does not mean the system will simply break. Scientists and institutions will adapt. But we should be honest about the scale of the challenge. The entire apparatus for evaluating research and researchers — metrics, journals, grant review, promotion panels — was designed for a world in which scientific output was produced at human speed by human minds. That world is ending. What replaces it needs to be designed deliberately, not improvised under pressure.

10. 复合误差问题10. The Compounding Error Problem

科学方法的核心是捕捉错误的机器。它的自我修正特性依赖于一个 内在要求：产生结果的代理和检查结果的代理在很大程度上是独立的—— 不同的装备，不同的训练，不同的制度激励。人工管道中的错误主要是 不相关，并且它们的张力表面旗标。The scientific method is, at its core, a machine for catching mistakes. Its self-correcting property rests on one intrinsic requirement: that the agents producing results and the agents checking them are largely independent — different equipment, different training, different institutional incentives. Errors in a human pipeline are largely uncorrelated, and their tension surfaces flags.

闭环人工智能管道同时打破了每个阶段的这种独立性。假设生成，实验 设计、数据收集、分析和验证均由共享相同基础模型的代理处理， 相同的训练数据，相同的系统盲点。在第一阶段进入的偏见并没有遇到真正的问题 第三阶段的独立检查——它遇到了一个共享其假设的系统。错误不会取消；他们 在整个周期中默默地复合。A closed-loop AI pipeline breaks this independence at every stage simultaneously. Hypothesis generation, experimental design, data collection, analysis, and validation are all handled by agents sharing the same foundation models, the same training data, the same systematic blind spots. A bias that enters at stage one does not encounter a genuinely independent check at stage three — it encounters a system that shares its assumptions. The errors do not cancel; they compound silently across the full cycle.

这是复制危机的人工智能类似物，但从根本上来说更糟糕。在心理学或医学中， 共同的方法论假设最终遇到了现实和独立复制的阻力。 在大规模人工智能中 科学管道，两种纠正机制同时被削弱。体积使人类能够独立复制 不可能获得超过一小部分的结果。以及人工智能到人工智能的复制，如果它共享相同的底层 架构产生的不是独立的测试，而是回声——一种没有实质内容的验证形式。This is the AI analogue of the replication crisis, but fundamentally worse. In psychology or medicine, shared methodological assumptions eventually met the resistance of reality and independent replication. In a large-scale AI science pipeline, both corrective mechanisms are weakened simultaneously. Volume makes independent human replication impossible for more than a fraction of results. And AI-to-AI replication, if it shares the same underlying architecture, produces not an independent test but an echo — the form of verification without its substance.

进一步的危险是重新摄入。随着新结果的积累，人工智能系统会被重新训练。有缺陷的结果被吸收到 下一代的训练语料库不再是一个孤立的错误——它成为先验的，塑造后续的 假设和实验设计。错误被制度化而不是被纠正，并且管道发生了漂移 默默地远离经验现实，似乎没有任何一步导致它。The further danger is reingestion. AI systems are retrained as new results accumulate. A flawed result absorbed into the next generation’s training corpus does not remain an isolated error — it becomes a prior, shaping subsequent hypotheses and experimental designs. The error is institutionalized rather than corrected, and the pipeline drifts silently away from empirical reality with no single step appearing to cause it.

这个问题最深刻的版本是最基本的：谁读取物理现实的原始输出？在所有科学中， 最终的检查不是同行评审，而是与大自然的接触——光谱、显微镜图像、探测器信号、患者 结果与预测不符。管道自动化程度越高，这种联系的中介程度就越高 变成。在一个首先针对速度进行优化的系统中，谁来验证验证者的问题不是一个技术问题 细节。这是人工智能驱动的科学的核心认识论挑战，但尚未得到足够的重视。 回答。The deepest version of this problem is the most basic: who reads the raw output of physical reality? In all science, the ultimate check is not peer review but contact with nature — the spectrum, the microscope image, the detector signal, the patient outcome that refuses to match the prediction. The more the pipeline is automated, the more mediated this contact becomes. The question of who verifies the verifiers, in a system optimized above all for speed, is not a technical detail. It is the central epistemological challenge of AI-driven science, and it has not yet received an adequate answer.

从最精确的意义上来说，人工智能驱动的科学提出的是What AI-driven science proposes is, in the most precise sense of the term, the研究产业化industrialization of research——随着工业化历史上的发展 带来并可能带来：生产力、影响力和影响力方面的非凡收益 跨领域重组、资本集中、标准化 产出，以及不适合新模式的知识形式的逐渐侵蚀 生产。以前的工业革命并未停止，但其后果已经形成 取决于他们在部署过程中所做的选择，这些选择就是本文的内容 尝试绘制地图。— with all that industrialization has historically brought and may yet bring: extraordinary gains in productivity, reach, and cross-domain recombination, alongside concentration of capital, standardization of output, and the gradual erosion of forms of knowledge that do not fit the new mode of production. Previous industrial revolutions were not stopped — but their consequences were shaped by the choices made during their deployment, and those choices are what this essay has tried to map.

这里提出的担忧并不能得出单一的结论。有些人会把它们视为质疑的理由 人工智能驱动的科学发现——创世纪就是一个突出的例子。这里采取的立场是 不同：核心问题不是是否追求人工智能驱动的科学，而是在什么条件下它会实现 兑现其承诺。 科学能力的代际传递会发生什么变化？ 基础设施可以替代吗？ 当理论出现时我们保留什么样的理解 变得太不透明以至于人类无法解释？ 人工智能能否带来范式转变 定义了科学最具变革性的发现——或者只是加速了科学的发展 增量？ 谁控制着所提出的问题——比如气候的缺失 创世纪优先事项的科学已经表明？ 研究人员会发生什么 他们的工作被他们无法访问的系统吸收，从而提高了生产力 他们永远不会受益？ 怎么办 当数量激增且人工智能审查人工智能时，我们会评估质量和研究人员吗？ 当管道产生错误时，谁捕获错误 结果和验证它们的流程是否具有相同的系统偏差？ 这些不是外围细节。它们是改变科学的基础设施与改变科学的基础设施之间的区别。 这只会加速其最坏的趋势。The concerns raised here do not lead to a single conclusion. Some will read them as reasons to question AI-driven science discovery altogether — of which Genesis is one prominent example. The position taken here is different: the central question is not whether to pursue AI-driven science, but under what conditions it will deliver on its promises. What happens to the intergenerational transmission of scientific competence that no infrastructure can replace? What kind of understanding do we preserve when theories become too opaque for human minds to interpret? Can AI produce the paradigm-shifting discoveries that have defined science at its most transformative — or only accelerate the incremental? Who controls the questions being asked — as the absence of climate science from Genesis’s priorities already shows? And what happens to the researchers whose work is absorbed by systems they cannot access, contributing to a productivity they will never benefit from? How do we evaluate quality — and researchers — when quantity explodes and AI reviews AI? Who catches the errors when the pipeline that produces results and the pipeline that validates them share the same systematic biases? These are not peripheral details. They are the difference between an infrastructure that transforms science and one that merely accelerates its worst tendencies.

这些不是旨在减缓进展的反问句。它们是真正的开放式问题和答案 将塑造在这一转变的另一边出现的科学文化。一门更快、更多的科学 富有成效，但不太容易理解，没有得到很好的评估，也不太有能力培训下一代，并且 结构上分散在资源丰富的人工智能层和其他所有人之间——并不比什么更好 我们现在有。要做到这一点，不仅需要工程雄心，还需要认真、持续地讨论什么 科学是为了——也是我们不想在加速科学发展的过程中失去的东西。These are not rhetorical questions designed to slow down progress. They are genuine open questions, and the answers will shape the scientific culture that emerges on the other side of this transition. A science that is faster and more productive, but less comprehensible, less well-evaluated, less capable of training the next generation, and structurally fragmented between a well-resourced AI tier and everyone else — is not straightforwardly better than what we have now. Getting this right requires not just engineering ambition, but a serious, ongoing conversation about what science is for — and what we do not want to lose in the process of accelerating it.
