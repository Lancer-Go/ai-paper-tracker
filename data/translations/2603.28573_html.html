摘要Abstract

Partial Action Replacement (PAR) mitigates this by anchoring a subset of agents to dataset actions, but existing approach relies on enumerating multiple subset configurations at high computational cost and cannot adapt to varying states. We introduce PLCQL, a framework that formulates PAR subset selection as a contextual bandit problem and learns a state-dependent PAR policy using Proximal Policy Optimisation with an uncertainty-weighted reward. This adaptive policy dynamically determines how many agents to replace at each update step, balancing policy improvement against conservative value estimation. We prove a value-error bound showing that the estimation error scales linearly with the expected number of deviating agents. Compared with the previous PAR-based method SPaCQL, PLCQL reduces the number of per-iteration Q-function evaluations fromnn to 11，显着提高计算效率。根据经验，PLCQL 在 MPE、MaMuJoCo 和 SMAC 基准测试中，在 66% 的任务上取得了最高标准化分数，在 84% 的任务上优于 SPaCQL，同时大幅降低了计算成本。, significantly improving computational efficiency. Empirically, PLCQL achieves the highest normalised scores on 66% of tasks across MPE, MaMuJoCo, and SMAC benchmarks, outperforming SPaCQL on 84% of tasks while substantially reducing computational cost.

1 简介Introduction

离线多智能体强化学习（MARL）旨在从固定数据集中学习协调策略，而无需与环境进行额外交互。此设置对于在线探索成本高昂、不安全或不切实际的现实应用程序特别有吸引力，例如机器人、自治系统和大规模资源管理Offline multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) aims to learn coordinated policies from fixed datasets without additional interaction with the environment. This setting is particularly attractive for real-world applications where online exploration is costly, unsafe, or impractical, such as robotics, autonomous systems, and large-scale resource management[19, 11, 15, 23]。然而，离线 MARL 引入了一个比单智能体环境更为严峻的根本挑战：联合行动空间的组合爆炸。随着智能体数量的增加，可能的联合动作的数量呈指数级增长，而任何固定的数据集只能覆盖这些组合的一小部分。因此，学习算法必须估计许多分布外（OOD）联合动作的价值。当与神经网络等函数逼近器结合时，基于值的方法可能会为这些看不见的动作分配任意大的 Q 值，从而导致严重的高估和利用值错误而不是有效协调的策略. However, offline MARL introduces a fundamental challenge that is significantly more severe than in the single-agent setting: the combinatorial explosion of the joint-action space. As the number of agents increases, the number of possible joint actions grows exponentially, while any fixed dataset can cover only a small fraction of these combinations. Consequently, learning algorithms must estimate the value of many out-of-distribution (OOD) joint actions. When combined with function approximators such as neural networks, value-based methods may assign arbitrarily large Q-values to these unseen actions, leading to severe overestimation and policies that exploit value errors rather than effective coordination[25, 22, 9, 8].

缓解这一问题的一个有前途的策略是限制策略更新以保持接近数据集的支持。最近的工作引入了部分动作替换（PAR），它仅更改代理子集的动作，同时将其余动作固定为数据集中观察到的动作One promising strategy for mitigating this problem is to constrain policy updates to remain close to the support of the dataset. Recent work introduces partial action replacement (PAR), which changes the actions of only a subset of agents while keeping the remaining actions fixed to those observed in the dataset[5]。通过将部分联合行动锚定到数据支持的行动上，PAR 减少了分配转移并稳定了离线政策学习。然而，PAR 的有效性关键取决于选择哪个智能体子集来进行动作替换。现有方法，例如软部分保守 Q 学习 (SPaCQL)，尝试通过枚举多个子集大小并为每个大小随机采样组合来解决此问题，然后对结果目标值采用基于不确定性的加权方案. By anchoring part of the joint action to data-supported actions, PAR reduces distributional shift and stabilizes offline policy learning. However, the effectiveness of PAR critically depends on which subset of agents is selected for action replacement. Existing methods, such as Soft Partial Conservative Q-Learning (SPaCQL), attempt to address this issue by enumerating multiple subset sizes and randomly sampling combinations for each size, followed by an uncertainty-based weighting scheme over the resulting target values[5]。尽管这种方法可以提高鲁棒性，但它会带来大量的计算开销，因为必须在每个更新步骤中评估多个 PAR 配置。此外，基于不确定性的加权机制没有明确优先考虑导致更强的多智能体协调的子集。因此，现有的基于 PAR 的方法依赖于计算成本高昂的枚举，同时缺乏选择协调有效子集的原则机制。. Although this approach can improve robustness, it introduces substantial computational overhead because multiple PAR configurations must be evaluated at every update step. Moreover, the uncertainty-based weighting mechanism does not explicitly prioritize subsets that lead to stronger multi-agent coordination. As a result, existing PAR-based approaches rely on computationally expensive enumeration while lacking a principled mechanism for selecting coordination-effective subsets.

We formulate the problem as a contextual bandit, where the PAR policy maps states to subset sizes and receives an uncertainty-weighted reward derived from the estimated joint-action value. This encourages both high-value coordination and conservative, reliable value estimation. We propose PAR Learning-based Conservative Q-Learning (PLCQL), which jointly trains the MARL policy and the PAR strategy from offline data. By sampling a single PAR subset per update step rather than enumerating all configurations, PLCQL substantially reduces computational cost while retaining the benefits of partial action anchoring. To our knowledge, this is the first work to formulate PAR subset selection as a learning problem in offline MARL.
