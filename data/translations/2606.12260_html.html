人工智能的市场设计：超越版权二进制Market Design for AI: Beyond the Copyright Binary

Yan Dai111MIT Operations Research Center. yandai20@mit.edu.   Maryam Farboodi222MIT Sloan School of Management, NBER and CEPR. farboodi@mit.edu.   Negin Golrezaei333MIT Sloan School of Management. golrezae@mit.edu.   Sepehr Shahshahani444Washington University School of Law. sepehrs@wustl.edu.
摘要Abstract

我们如何设计一个用于训练人工智能模型的人类生成内容市场，既能促进技术进步，又能保留个人对高质量内容创作的激励？ 现有方法采取两极立场：基于合理使用的“所有人免费”模式和“强大的知识产权”模式。 我们证明两者都失败了：混战并不能补偿创作者，而且通过建模为静态斯塔克伯格博弈，强大的知识产权也会削弱创造性激励。我们发现对于更具创新性的创作者来说尤其如此，我们将这种现象称为“原创性惩罚”。 将这一见解扩展到动态模型，我们发现另一个市场失灵会破坏人工智能模型的性能，即使对于最初良好的模型也是如此：这种模型会导致人类对人工智能辅助创作的更大依赖，导致反馈到训练中的内容同质化，从而降低了模型性能——这是“精度的诅咒”。 我们进一步提出了一种市场设计，其中数据中介将跨创造者的外部性内部化并补贴创新贡献，从而恢复效率。How can we design a market of human-generated content for use in training AI models that both enables technological progress and preserves individual incentives for high-quality content creation? Existing approaches take polar positions: a “free-for-all” model based on fair use and a “strong intellectual property rights” model. We show that both fail: Free-for-all does not compensate creators, and—by modeling as a static Stackelberg game—strong intellectual property rights also underpower creative incentives. We find this especially true for more innovative creators, a phenomenon we term the “originality penalty.” Extending this insight to a dynamic model, we find another market failure undermining AI model performance, even for an initially good model: Such a model induces greater reliance by humans on AI-assisted creation, resulting in homogenized content feeding back into training, which degrades the model performance—a “curse of precision.” We further propose a market design with a data intermediary internalizing cross-creator externalities and subsidizing innovative contributions, thereby restoring efficiency.

1 简介Introduction

生成人工智能 (AI) 领域的最新突破既充满希望，也充满危险。 通过快速、廉价地交付有用内容，人工智能模型改善了数百万人的工作和生活。 但人工智能模型生成这些有用内容的能力取决于丰富的人类创建内容可供训练。 迄今为止，人工智能公司通过从在线资源中抓取这些内容来获取这些内容，很大程度上没有经过人类创作者的同意、补偿或信用。 虽然这种做法促进了人工智能的快速进步，但它削弱了人类创造者的创造动力。 我们已经看到了警告信号：在 ChatGPT 等大型语言模型 (LLM) 兴起后，Stack Overflow 的流量大幅下降，因为用户消费了人工智能答案，而不是为生成答案做出贡献Recent breakthroughs in generative artificial intelligence (AI) are laden with both promise and peril. By enabling the fast, cheap delivery of useful content, AI models have improved work and life for millions of people. But AI models’ ability to generate this useful content depends on a rich reservoir of human-created content on which to train. To date, AI firms have acquired this content by scraping it from online sources—largely without human creators’ consent, compensation, or credit. While this practice has enabled rapid AI progress, it undermines human creators’ incentives to create. We are already seeing warning signs: Stack Overflow’s traffic declined significantly after the rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT, as users consumed AI answers rather than contributing to producing them(burtch2024consequences; del2024large; sharma2025beyond)。 各种艺术家和其他内容创作者起诉人工智能公司，声称他们的做法剥夺了他们创造性工作的回报（参见. And various artists and other content creators have sued AI firms contending that their practices have deprived them of the rewards of their creative work (seeSection 2）。 这些趋势表明人工智能开发人员和人类创造者的激励不一致，并引发了该项目的核心研究问题： 我们如何为人工智能设计一个人类生成内容的市场，既能促进技术进步，又能保持个人创造高质量内容的动力？). These trends point to a misalignment between the incentives of AI developers and human creators, and they give rise to the central research question of this project: How can we design a market of human-generated content for AI that both enables technological progress and preserves individuals’ incentive to create high-quality content?

这是我们这个时代最重要的政策挑战之一，涉及创新和技术政策的重要问题，更广泛地说，涉及如何保护人类创造力蓬勃发展的根本问题。 但我们拥有的工具来自几十年前的版权法，该法在设计时并没有考虑到人工智能技术之类的东西。 因此，当前的争论陷入了法律二元论：人工智能收获要么是合理使用（我们称之为“所有人免费”的系统），要么是侵犯版权（“严格的知识产权”制度）。 本文认为，这两个极端都注定会失败，并指出了通过数据中介的“第三条道路”。This is among the foremost policy challenges of our times, implicating in its narrow berth important questions of innovation and technology policy and, more broadly, fundamental concerns about how to preserve human creative flourishing. But the tools we have come from a decades-old Copyright Act that was not designed with anything like AI technology in mind. As such, the current debate is trapped in a legal binary: AI harvesting is either fair use (what we call a “free for all” system) or copyright infringement (a “strict intellectual property rights” regime). This paper argues that both extremes are destined to fail and points instead to a “third way” via data intermediaries.

混战系统失败的原因很容易理解： 如果人工智能可以自由地获取人类创造的内容，并用它来生成在很大程度上取代消费者对人类创造的内容的需求的内容，那么人类投资高质量内容创作的动力就会大大减弱。 但我们表明，相反的解决方案——严格的知识产权——也注定会失败。The reason a free-for-all system fails is easy to grasp: If AI can freely take human-created content and use it to generate content that largely replaces consumers’ need for the human-created content, then humans will be left with much-diminished incentives to invest in high-quality content creation. But we show that the opposite solution—strict intellectual property rights—is also doomed to fail.

这次失败的原因是微妙且出乎意料的。我们从静态 Stackelberg 模型开始，捕捉人类创造者和人工智能公司之间的互动。 首先，与关于知识产权影响的传统观点相反，我们表明，强烈的个人主义知识产权会导致创造性激励不足。 这是由于人工智能公司的市场力量以及不同人类创作者内容之间的相关性： 具体来说，市场力量允许人工智能公司将内容定价为低于促使人类创造者做出社会最优努力所需的价格，从而获利；内容相关性意味着，通过将自己的内容出售给人工智能公司，创作者实际上也在出售其他创作者的“部分”内容，这是一种以信息泄露形式出现的负外部性，压低了内容的价格。 其次，更令人惊讶的是，我们表明人工智能公司对代表性数据的偏爱会造成“原创性惩罚”，这意味着市场在更大程度上（相对于社会最优）降低了创新内容创作者的激励，而不是降低了受共同趋势和偏见高度影响的普通创作者的激励。 因此，这种原创性的损失导致了同质化内容的产生。 尽管可以预见，人工智能公司的市场力量可能会导致人类创造者投资不足，但我们对原创性惩罚的发现与稀缺性需要溢价的经济直觉相矛盾，揭示了人工智能背景下特有的市场失灵。The reasons for this failure are subtle and unexpected. We begin with a static Stackelberg model capturing the interaction between human creators and the AI firm. First, contrary to conventional wisdom about the effects of intellectual property (IP) rights, we show that strong individualistic IP rights result in under-powered creative incentives. This is due to the market power of AI firms and correlation among different human creators’ content: Specifically, market power allows an AI firm to profitably price content below what would be required to induce socially optimal effort on the part of human creators; content correlation implies that, by selling her own content to the AI firm, a creator is also effectively selling “part of” other creators’ content, a negative externality in the form of information leakage that depresses the price of content. Second—and more surprisingly—we show that AI firm’s preference for representative data creates an “originality penalty,” meaning the market degrades the incentives of innovative content creators to a greater extent (relative to social optimum) than it degrades the incentives of run-of-the-mill creators who are highly influenced by common trends and biases. This originality penalty thus leads to the production of homogenized content. Although it is predictable that AI firms’ market power may lead to under-investment by human creators, our finding of an originality penalty contradicts the economic intuition that scarcity demands a premium, revealing a market failure that is distinctive to the AI context.

除了人类创造性激励和技术进步之间的短期权衡之外，我们还进一步将分析扩展到连续时间动态环境，其中人工智能公司随着时间的推移训练和完善人类生成内容的模型，而人类反过来使用人工智能工具来帮助他们生成内容。 在这种动态环境中，我们发现了“精度诅咒”，即良好的模型会导致人类内容创建者对人工智能输出的更大依赖，导致人类内容同质化，从而反馈到人工智能训练管道中，从而降低模型性能。 意想不到的后果是，一个好的模型播下了自身衰败的种子。 虽然这种“精度诅咒”看起来与实证机器学习文献中发现的“模型崩溃”现象相似Beyond the short-run tradeoff between human creative incentives and technological progress, we further extend our analysis to a continuous-time dynamic setting in which an AI firm trains and refines its models on human-generated content over time, and humans in turn use AI tools to assist them generating content. In this dynamic setting, we identify a “curse of precision” whereby a good model induces greater reliance by human content creators on AI outputs, leading to a homogenization of human content, which feed back into the AI training pipeline, degrading model performance. The unexpected consequence is that a good model sows the seeds of its own decay. While this “curse of precision” looks similar to the phenomenon of “model collapse” identified in the empirical machine learning literature(alemohammad2023self; shumailov2023curse)——当使用自我生成的数据进行训练时，人工智能模型的能力就会崩溃——然而，我们的分析揭示了一条不同的失败途径和更悲观的预测： 即使人工智能公司不断从人类创造者那里获取新数据，设计不当的市场也可能导致人工智能模型失败。—when trained on self-generated data, an AI model’s capability collapses—our analysis, however, reveals a different pathway to failure and a more pessimistic prediction: Even if the AI firm constantly acquires fresh data from human creators, poorly designed markets can lead to AI model failure.

在确定了现有制度设计下市场均衡的静态和动态特性后，我们通过提出更好的市场设计从诊断转向治疗。 我们提出的解决方案采用数据中介的形式 代表个人内容所有者与人工智能公司进行谈判，并在他们之间分配版税。 我们表明，在提供完整信息的情况下，数据中介可以克服个人知识产权制度所特有的市场失灵。 通过将创作者作为一个投资组合进行管理并代表他们进行集体谈判，中介机构既克服了人工智能公司的市场力量，又将数据关联和信息泄露所产生的负外部性内部化。 通过根据个人创作者各自对社会盈余的贡献（使用我们根据奥曼-沙普利值定义的权重）分配从人工智能公司收到的付款，中介机构扭转了人工智能公司和人类内容创作者之间无中介互动的原创性惩罚。 有趣的是，我们表明，可以使用简单的两部分关税机制来恢复效率，其中中介机构从人工智能公司收到的一次性转移和中介机构向个人内容创作者支付的金额都是内容创作者所付出努力的仿射函数。Having identified the static and dynamic properties of market equilibrium under existing institutional designs, we then move beyond diagnosis to treatment by proposing a better market design. Our proposed solution takes the form of a data intermediary who negotiates on behalf of individual content owners with an AI firm and apportions royalties among them. We show that, given full information, the data intermediary can overcome both market failures that characterize the individual-IP-rights regime. By managing creators as a portfolio and negotiating collectively on their behalf, the intermediary both overcomes the AI firm’s market power and internalizes the negative externality arising from data correlation and information leakage. And by apportioning the payment received from the AI firm among individual creators according to their respective contributions to social surplus (using weights that we define inspired by the Aumann–Shapley value), the intermediary reverses the originality penalty that characterizes unintermediated interactions between AI firms and human content creators. We show, interestingly, that efficiency can be restored using a simple two-part tariff mechanism where both the lump-sum transfer that the intermediary receives from the AI firm and the amounts that the intermediary pays to individual content creators are affine functions of the effort exerted by content creators.

我们的中介解决方案抽象了信息不对称，更一般地说，抽象了中介和人类创造者之间的委托代理问题。 因此，它不应被视为最终的政策解决方案。 尽管如此，作为实现最佳市场设计的第一步，这个想法非常有用，因为任何表征全信息环境的问题也会困扰不完全信息环境。 即使没有信息差距或委托代理成本，我们识别的市场失灵也将存在，而它们正是我们提出的市场设计所解决的失灵，因此该解决方案针对所定义的问题进行了很好的校准，并作为最终解决方案的有用基准。Our intermediary solution abstracts away from informational asymmetries and, more generally, from principal-agent problems between the intermediary and human creators. As such, it should not be viewed as a definitive policy solution. The idea is nevertheless quite useful as a first step toward an optimal market design because any problems that characterize the full-information context will also plague an incomplete-information environment. The market failures we identify will exist even in the absence of information gaps or principal-agent costs, and they are the very failures addressed by our proposed market design, so the solution is well-calibrated to the problem as defined and serves as a useful benchmark for a definitive solution.

2 政策辩论和文献Policy Debate and Literature

如何最好地解决人工智能对信息内容创建和消费市场的干扰问题，不仅在学术文献中，而且在法庭上也引起了激烈的争论。 影响人工智能未来的最重要的法律问题已经在内容所有者针对人工智能公司提起的几起涉嫌侵犯版权的诉讼中提出。The question of how best to resolve AI’s disruption of the market for creating and consuming informational content is hotly debated not just in academic literature but also in courts. The most consequential legal questions affecting the future of AI have been raised in several lawsuits filed by content owners against AI firms alleging copyright infringement.555有用的列表出现在A useful list appears athttps://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2024/08/27/master-list-of-lawsuits-v-ai-chatgpt-openai-microsoft-meta-midjourney-other-ai-cos/。 对于不适合律师的介绍，请参阅. For a non-lawyer-friendly introduction, seesamuelson2023; samuelson2025. 对于外行人来说，版权与尖端技术的并置可能显得不寻常——毕竟，版权被认为与艺术创造力有关，而高科技环境似乎更适合专利法或其他监管制度——但事实上，版权法长期以来一直处于美国监管新技术的最前沿。 从钢琴卷帘到电缆、录像机、点对点文件共享、在线视频流等，决定新兴技术命运的第一步通常是由法院在版权案件中采取的To a layperson, the juxtaposition of copyright with the cutting edge of technology may appear unusual—after all, copyright is thought to be about artistic creativity, whereas the high-technology context might seem better suited to patent law or other regulatory regimes—but in fact copyright law has long been at the forefront of regulating new technologies in the United States. From the piano roll to cable to VCRs to peer-to-peer file sharing to online video streaming and beyond, the first steps in deciding the fate of emerging technologies have often been taken by courts in copyright cases(shahshahani2018; samuelson2024)。 要了解其中的原因，有必要回顾一下美国知识产权法的基本结构，其中版权是其中的核心部分。. To appreciate the reasons for this, it is essential to review the fundamental structure of American IP laws, of which copyright is a central part.

用美国宪法的话来说，美国知识产权法的目标是“通过在有限的时间内确保作者和发明人的专有权利来促进科学和实用艺术的进步”。 他们各自的著作和发现”（美国宪法第 I 条，第 8 条，第 8 条）。 这种“排他性权利”（换句话说，暂时的垄断）被认为是实现艺术和科学进步目标所必需的，其原因可以追溯到精神无形产品的“公共利益”本质： 与有形商品（例如一把椅子）不同，无形产品具有“非竞争性”（一个人的使用不会减少另一个人的使用）和“非排他性”（一个想法一旦被披露，其使用就很难仅限于第一个接受者）。 因此，美国知识产权法的理论是，专有权对于防止搭便车他人创造力并从而保持创造动力是必要的。 但该理论也承认这些专有权是有代价的： 与任何垄断一样，知识产权会增加受知识产权保护的作品的成本，并使用户更难以访问。 其中不仅包括最终用户的成本，例如想要阅读受版权保护的小说或服用专利药物的人，还包括在人工智能背景下特别重要的后续创作者想要使用现有内容作为创建新内容的构建块的成本The goal of American IP laws, in the words of the U.S. Constitution, is “to promote the progress of science and useful arts, by securing for limited times to authors and inventors the exclusive right to their respective writings and discoveries” (U.S. Const. art. I, § 8, cl. 8). The reason such an “exclusive right”—in other words, a temporary monopoly—has been considered necessary to achieve the objective of progress in arts and science traces to the “public good” nature of intangible products of the mind: Unlike tangible goods (say, a chair), intangible products are “nonrivalrous”—one person’s use does not diminish another person’s use—and “nonexclusive”—the use of an idea once disclosed is hard to limit to the first recipient. As such, the theory of American IP laws is that exclusive rights are necessary to prevent freeriding on others’ creativity and thus maintain incentives to create. But the theory also acknowledges that these exclusive rights come at a cost: Like any monopoly, IP rights raise the cost of a work protected by IP and make it more difficult for users to access. 在本节中，我们列出了在我们继续完善市场设计提案时需要解决的一些剩余问题和挑战。 如上所述，我们的解决方案的必要性源于法院通常可用的两种选择的失败。 无论是通过合理使用实现全面豁免的制度还是完全控制版权所有者对内容访问的法律制度都不足以保留创造性激励，因此可行的解决方案必须超越传统的版权二元论。 尽管我们在这种情况下对市场失灵的诊断是新颖的，但我们关于超越共同版权二元的建议绝不是没有先例的。 美国版权法（更不用说其他国家的法律）包含许多此类“中间”制度。In this section, we lay out some remaining questions and challenges to be addressed as we continue to refine our market design proposal. As discussed above, the need for our solution emerges out of the failure of the two options usually available to courts. Neither a system of blanket immunity via fair use nor a legal regime of full control over access to content by copyright owners will be sufficient to preserve creative incentives, so a workable solution must transcend the traditional copyright binary. Although our diagnosis of market failures in this context is novel, our proposal for going beyond the common copyright binary is by no means without precedent. The first question relates to the compulsory or voluntary character of participation in an intermediary organization: Once the intermediary is constructed, can its benefits be realized by content creators’ uncoordinated, self-interested equilibrium behavior or do collective-action problems require centralized enforcement and a legal obligation to join? 6 结论Conclusion

本文研究了人工智能数据市场的经济学，并将创作者内容之间的相关性所产生的统计可替代性确定为阻碍人工智能公司与人类创作者之间有效讨价还价的核心摩擦。在静态市场中，我们表明，相关性和人工智能公司市场力量之间的相互作用会产生原创性惩罚，这种惩罚会严重损害生产不相关且具有社会价值内容的创作者，从而削弱对边际贡献最高的创作者的激励。将分析扩展到人工智能辅助创作的动态环境中，我们发现了精度的诅咒：人工智能模型的改进导致数据生产日益同质化，形成市场价格上限，并可能导致创新创作者退出。因此，如果没有适当的市场设计，即使不断流入新的人类数据也可能无法防止长期模型崩溃。This paper studies the economics of data markets for AI and identifies statistical substitutability arising from correlation across creators’ content as the central friction preventing efficient bargaining between AI firms and human creators. In static markets, we show that the interaction between correlation and AI firms’ market power generates an originality penalty that disproportionately harms creators producing uncorrelated and socially valuable content, thereby weakening incentives precisely for the creators with the highest marginal contribution. Extending the analysis to a dynamic setting with AI-assisted creation, we identify a curse of precision: improvements in AI models induce increasingly homogenized data production, creating an upper bound on market prices and potentially causing innovative creators to exit. As a result, even a continued inflow of fresh human data may fail to prevent long-run model collapse absent an appropriate market design.

为了解决这些失败，我们提出了一个由制度设计和契约设计两个部分组成的市场设计。特别是，我们提出了一种数据中介，它使用两部分关税合同来实现社会最优，同时满足参与约束并保持预算平衡。该机制通过将创新激励与技术进步结合起来，恢复模型绩效和人类创造力的长期参与。最后，我们强调了实施过程中剩下的两个挑战：中介组织的参与是否可以通过分散的均衡行为出现，还是需要集中执行和法定协调，以及中介机构如何在外部内容市场薄弱或不存在的环境中估计创作者的边际贡献。因此，将所提出的机制与数据评估技术相结合仍然是未来研究的重要方向。To address these failures, we propose a market design with two components, institutional design and contract design. In particular, we propose a data intermediary that uses a two-part tariff contract that implements the social optimum while satisfying participation constraints and maintaining budget balance. The mechanism restores long-run participation for both model performance and human creativity by aligning incentives for innovation with technological progress. We conclude by highlighting two remaining challenges for implementation: whether participation in intermediary organizations can emerge through decentralized equilibrium behavior or instead requires centralized enforcement and statutory coordination, and how intermediaries can estimate creators’ marginal contributions in environments where outside markets for content are thin or nonexistent. Integrating the proposed mechanism with data valuation techniques therefore remains an important direction for future research.
