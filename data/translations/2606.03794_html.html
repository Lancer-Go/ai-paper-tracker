摘要Abstract

图神经网络 (GNN) 已成为利用通信网络底层图结构的无线资源分配的强大工具。它们的可转移性特性使得在小规模图上训练的模型能够推广到大规模部署，而性能几乎没有下降，这对于当前不断增长的网络来说是一个理想的特性。无线网络是稀疏网络，其中单个节点连接到少量其他用户。这项工作为 GNN 在稀疏随机几何图 (RGG) 派生的图上的可迁移性建立了理论结果。我们特别关注用于建模链接之间干扰的 RGG 冲突图。我们的方法考虑了 RGG 和确定性网格图 (DGG) 之间的密切性，以确定跨尺度转移模型时性能损失的界限。我们通过链路调度问题验证了我们的理论发现，证明我们学习的策略在规模上始终优于现有基准。最后，我们研究了理论假设对实证表现的影响。Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for wireless resource allocation that leverages the underlying graph structure of communication networks. Their transferability property enables models trained on small-scale graphs to generalize to large-scale deployments with little performance deterioration, a desirable property for currently growing networks. Wireless networks are sparse regimes, where a single node is connected to a small number of other users. This work establishes theoretical results for transferability of GNNs over graphs derived from sparse Random Geometric Graphs (RGGs). In particular, we focus on conflict graphs of RGGs used to model interference among links. Our approach considers the closeness between RGGs and Deterministic Grid Graphs (DGG) to establish bounds in the performance loss when a model is transferred across scales. We validate our theoretical findings through the problem of link scheduling, demonstrating that our learned policies consistently outperform existing benchmarks at scale. Finally, we examine the impact of our theoretical assumptions on empirical performance.

To bridge this gap, recent work has begun analyzing transferability on sparse graphs via graphops[8]或使用随机几何图（RGG），利用通信系统固有的几何结构or using Random Geometric Graphs (RGGs), leveraging the geometric structure intrinsic to communication systems[3].

VI 结论Conclusions

随机几何图是一种简单而准确的无线网络模型。这项工作提供了图神经网络在稀疏 RGG 派生的冲突图上可迁移性的理论研究。这确保了经过训练以满足网络链路要求的无线策略可以转移到更大的图表，同时性能损失最小。我们通过无线链路调度任务的数值实验验证了我们的理论结果。更好地评估我们的假设与实际发展的相关性仍然至关重要，我们将其留给未来的工作。Random Geometric Graphs are a simple, yet accurate model of wireless networks. This work provides the theoretical study of transferability of Graph Neural Networks on conflict graphs derived from sparse RGG. This ensures wireless policies trained to satisfy requirements on the links of a network can be transferred to larger graphs with minimal loss in performance. We verified our theoretical results hold with numerical experiments on the wireless link scheduling task. It remains crucial to better assess the relevance of our assumptions with respect to practical development, which we leave for future work.
