摘要Abstract

同行评审中的科学图形评估与一般图像质量评估有根本不同：图形必须视觉上清晰，忠实支持手稿的主张，并以清晰的视觉层次传达证据。然而，如果我们将传统的图像评估方法应用于科学图形质量评估，就会出现局限性：经典的 IQA 模型捕捉到感知质量或美学，但无法判断图形是否符合论文的科学论点；基于 CLIP 的方法评估一般图像-文本对应关系，但缺乏对手稿上下文的理解；零样本的 LLM/VLM 评委在重新用于数字评分时，通常会产生过于集中的分数，而视觉和文本证据的融合有限。我们引入了来自同行评审会议论文的 3,857 个科学数据的带注释数据集，每个数据集都按照四个同行评审导向的维度进行评级：清晰度、相关性、信息性和结构。我们建议Scientific figure assessment in peer review differs fundamentally from general image quality evaluation: a figure must be visually legible, faithfully support the manuscript’s claims, and communicate evidence with a clear visual hierarchy. However, if we apply traditional image assessment methods to scientific figure quality assessment, limitations emerge: classic IQA models capture perceptual quality or aesthetics but cannot judge whether a figure serves the paper’s scientific argument; CLIP-based methods assess generic image-text correspondence, yet lack understanding of manuscript context; and zero-shot LLM/VLM judges, when repurposed for figure scoring, often yield overly concentrated scores with limited fusion of visual and textual evidence. We introduce an annotated dataset of 3,857 scientific figures from peer-reviewed conference papers, each rated along four peer-review-oriented dimensions: Clarity, Relevance, Informativeness, and Structure. 1 简介Introduction

一个精心设计的人物可以在很大程度上影响一篇论文的实证叙述，但评估其质量比评估一张日常照片要复杂得多。科学数据必须在印刷尺寸下保持可读性，忠实于周围的主张，并以清晰的视觉层次呈现证据A single well-designed figure can largely impact a paper’s empirical narrative, and yet assessing its quality is far more complex than evaluating an everyday photograph. Scientific figures must remain readable at print scale, stay faithful to surrounding claims, and present evidence with a clear visual hierarchy(Borkin et al. 2013; Wang et al. 2004)。 在同行评审中，很少单独对一个数字进行评判。审稿人总是会问标签是否清晰，视觉效果是否支持标题和引用文本，情节是否为所声称的贡献提供了信息，以及面板是否允许连贯的阅读路径。 忽视这些要求的自动评分方法对于审稿助理、创作工具或科学论文的语料库级分析的价值有限。. In peer review, a figure is rarely judged on its own. Reviewers always ask whether labels are legible, whether the visual supports the caption and citing text, whether the plot is informative for the claimed contribution, and whether panels admit a coherent reading path. Automatic scoring methods that disregard these requirements offer limited value for review assistants, authoring tools, or corpus-level analysis of scientific papers.

自然图像质量很大程度上取决于感知Natural-image quality is largely perceptual(Mittal et al. 2012)：风景照片通常可以直接从清晰度、色彩和美观度来判断。 然而，科学图表则不同：它们的效用取决于印刷版的可读性、标题的保真度以及所引用证据的诚实表述。 如果在文本声称“一致增益”的情况下轴被截断，或者如果架构示意图是装饰性的而不是为所要求的方法提供信息，则仅靠像素级检查无法暴露缺陷。 因此，评估科学论文数据是多模式的：它取决于内容、标题以及手稿在引用段落中主张的内容。: a landscape photograph can often be directly judged from sharpness, color, and aesthetics. However, scientific figures are different: their utility depends on print-scale legibility, caption fidelity, and honest representation of cited evidence. If axes are truncated while the text asserts “consistent gains,” or if an architecture schematic is decorative rather than informative for the claimed method, pixel-level inspection alone cannot expose the defect. Assessing scientific paper figures is therefore multimodal: it depends on the crop, the caption, and what the manuscript asserts in citing paragraphs.

3.5 培训目标Training Objective

图形质量既是论文中的绝对分数，也是相对判断。 人类评估者通常发现成对比较比将每个数字放在绝对比例上更自然，特别是当大多数发布的数字处于中高质量范围时。 因此，SciFigAlign 将逐点回归与论文内排名目标相结合。Figure quality is both an absolute score and a relative judgment within a paper. Human raters often find pairwise comparison more natural than placing every figure on an absolute scale, especially when most published figures occupy a mid–high quality range. Accordingly, SciFigAlign combines pointwise regression with a within-paper ranking objective.

5 结论Conclusion

我们介绍We introduce科幻对齐SciFigAlign，一个通过基于手稿的多模态融合和论文内排名进行科学图形质量评估的统一框架，并附有来自同行评审的计算机科学会议 PDF 的 3,857 个图形的带有标题注释的基准，用于系统评估。 我们的实验表明，最先进的 LLM/VLM 评委很难在真实的 PDF 裁剪上校准连续的清晰度/相关性/信息性/结构分数，并且论文内排序增益从根本上受到引用上下文绑定和去噪的限制，而不仅仅是提示工程。 通过 SciFigAlign，我们暴露了现有流程中的关键瓶颈，突出了孤立图像感知评分与基于手稿的同行评审效用之间的差距。 该框架为对提交内容中的数字进行排名和分类提供了基础，并且可扩展到 OCR 密集图、增强的信息性基础以及需要可审核的视觉文本对齐的更广泛的场所。, a unified framework for scientific figure quality assessment through manuscript-grounded multimodal fusion and within-paper ranking, accompanied by a rubric-annotated benchmark of 3,857 figures from peer-reviewed CS conference PDFs for systematic evaluation. Our experiments reveal that state-of-the-art LLM/VLM judges struggle to calibrate continuous Clarity/Relevance/Informativeness/Structure scores on authentic PDF crops, and that within-paper ordering gains are fundamentally constrained by citing-context binding and denoising rather than prompt engineering alone. Through SciFigAlign, we expose critical bottlenecks in existing pipelines, highlighting the gap between isolated-image perceptual scoring and manuscript-grounded peer-review utility. This framework provides a foundation for ranking and triaging figures within a submission, and is extendable to OCR-dense plots, enhanced Informativeness grounding, and broader venues requiring auditable visual–text alignment.
