通过学习的多模态潜在表示进行跨区域葡萄抗寒性预测Cross-Regional Grapevine Cold Hardiness Prediction via Learned Multimodal Latent Representations

William Solow    Paola Pesantez-Cabrera    Markus Keller    Lav Khot    Sandhya Saisubramanian    Alan Fern
摘要Abstract

在冰冻温度会损害休眠芽并降低季节性产量的地区，准确预测木本植物的抗寒性至关重要。现有的生物物理、混合和深度学习模型在对本地数据进行训练时表现出很高的预测准确性，但在很大程度上仍然存在问题Accurate daily predictions of cold hardiness in woody plants are critical in regions where freezing temperatures can damage dormant buds and reduce seasonal yield. Existing biophysical, hybrid, and deep learning models have shown high predictive accuracy when trained on local data but remain largely特定地点的site-specific。抗寒性数据的可用性有限，加上缺乏将抗寒性预测转移到新地区和品种的原则性方法，限制了这些方法的更广泛采用和实际应用，特别是在数据稀缺的地区。为了解决这些限制，我们提出了一种耐寒性预测框架，该框架通过学习的嵌入捕获特定区域的变化来学习可转移的潜在表示。为了能够对以前未见过的区域进行预测，我们从（1）品种和生长区域的文本描述以及（2）有限的历史观察中推断嵌入，支持零样本和少样本迁移。对北美六个地区的数据集进行的实验表明，我们的方法始终优于最先进的耐寒性预测方法，产生更准确的预测，并大大改善了向数据稀缺地区的传输。. The limited availability of cold hardiness data, coupled with the lack of principled methods for transferring cold hardiness predictions to new regions and cultivars, has limited the broader adoption and practical utility of these approaches, particularly in data-scarce regions. To address these limitations, we propose a cold hardiness prediction framework that learns a transferable latent representation by capturing region-specific variation through learned embeddings. To enable prediction in previously unseen regions, we infer embeddings from (1) text descriptions of the cultivar and growing region, and (2) limited historical observations, supporting both zero-shot and few-shot transfer. Experiments on datasets from six regions across North America demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art cold hardiness prediction methods, yielding more accurate predictions and substantially improving transfer to data-scarce regions.

Our framework is applicable to biophysical, deep learning, and hybrid cold hardiness models, allowing a unified comparison of transfer performance across these modeling paradigms.
