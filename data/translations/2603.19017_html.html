摘要Abstract

我们提出了 MULTITEMPBENCH，这是一个多语言时间推理基准，涵盖五种语言（英语、德语、中文、阿拉伯语和豪萨语）和多种日历约定（公历、回历和中国农历）的日期算术、时区转换和时间关系提取三项任务。 MULTITEMPBENCH 包含 15 个We present MULTITEMPBENCH, a multilingual temporal reasoning benchmark spanning three tasks, date arithmetic, time zone conversion, and temporal relation extraction across five languages (English, German, Chinese, Arabic, and Hausa) and multiple calendar conventions (Gregorian, Hijri, and Chinese Lunar). MULTITEMPBENCH contains 15,通过翻译 750 个精心策划的英语问题并将每个问题扩展为受控的日期格式变体而构建了 000 个示例。我们评估 20 个法学硕士并介绍000 examples built by translating 750 curated English questions and expanding each into controlled date-format variants. We evaluate 20 LLMs and introduce the多语言数据碎片率multilingual Date Fragmentation Ratio(mDFR)，根据人类严重程度评级以及内部时间表示的几何探测分析进行校准。我们发现时间制品的标记化质量是一个依赖于资源的瓶颈：在低资源语言和较少见的日历格式中，碎片会破坏年/月/日分离并导致准确性崩溃，而高资源设置通常对数字级分割具有鲁棒性。除了标记化之外，交叉混合效应回归表明，时间线性是高资源语言中时间推理的最强预测因子，而碎片化是低资源语言中更强的预测因子。 [1](mDFR), calibrated with human severity ratings, together with geometric-probing analyses of internal temporal representations. We find tokenisation quality of temporal artefacts is a resource-dependent bottleneck: in low-resource languages and rarer calendar formats, fragmentation disrupts Year/Month/Day separation and accuracy collapses, while highresource settings are often robust to digit-level splitting. Beyond tokenisation, crossed mixedeffects regression shows that temporal linearity is the strongest predictor of temporal reasoning in high-resource languages, whereas fragmentation is the stronger predictor in low-resource languages.[1]

4 我们的方法4 Our Approach

我们的目标是确定控制时间推理的潜在因素，然后评估这些因素在不同语言之间的差异。为此，我们提出了一个指标，称为多语言日期碎片率（mDFR），来衡量日期的标记化质量，然后引入时间几何来捕获内部时间表示的几何结构。Our aim is to identify the underlying factors that control temporal reasoning, then evaluate how these factors vary across languages. To do so, we present a metric, which we call the multilingual Date Fragmentation Ratio (mDFR), to measure the tokenisation quality of dates, then introduce temporal geometry to capture the geometric structures of internal temporal representations.

局限性Limitations

MULTITEMPBENCH 被设计为受控诊断，该设计对通用性施加了限制：它涵盖五种语言（英语、德语、中文、阿拉伯语和豪萨语）和三个任务族（日期算术、时区转换和时间关系提取），因此它不能完全代表多语言时间现象的多样性（例如，附加脚本和方言、代码混合/嘈杂文本、特定领域行话或超出这些范围的其他日历约定）；实例是通过翻译和来自策划的英语种子集的模板化格式变化生成的，这有助于隔离表面形式/标记化效果，但可能会对自然发生的表达式和错误分布进行采样不足；我们在零样本、直接答案的设置中进行评估（并以法学硕士作为法官对输出进行标准化），这提高了可比性，但可能低估了工具使用、提示或微调下的表现，并引入了来自法官错误和格式模糊性的残留评估噪声；虽然我们发现碎片指标、时间线性和性能之间存在很强的关联，但我们的机制分析仍然是相关的和以探针为中心的，从而留下了有关分词器设计、训练数据、解码动态和非线性表示结构的开放因果问题。我们的多语言设置的一个关键限制是，低资源制度仅由两种语言（阿拉伯语和豪萨语）代表，因此有关低资源时间推理的主张应被解释为暗示性的，而不是在低资源语言中完全通用的。更广泛地说，将语言分为高资源和低资源的语言必然是粗略的，并且需要其他语言来测试相同的瓶颈模式是否适用于其他类型配置文件、脚本和日历传统。MULTITEMPBENCH is designed as a controlled diagnostic, and that design imposes constraints on generality: it covers five languages (English, German, Chinese, Arabic, and Hausa) and three task families (date arithmetic, time zone conversion, and temporal relation extraction), so it does not fully represent the diversity of multilingual temporal phenomena (e.g., additional scripts and dialects, codemixing/noisy text, domain-specific jargon, or other calendar conventions beyond those included); instances are produced via translation and templated format variation from a curated English seed set, which helps isolate surface-form/tokenisation effects but may under-sample naturally occurring distributions of expressions and errors; we evaluate in a zero-shot, direct-answer setting (and normalise outputs with an LLM-as-a-judge), which improves comparability yet may understate performance under tool use, prompting, or fine-tuning and introduces residual evaluation noise from judge mistakes and format ambiguity; and while we find strong associations between fragmentation metrics, temporal linearity, and performance, our mechanistic analyses remain correlational and probe-centric, leaving open causal questions about tokeniser design, training data, decoding dynamics, and nonlinear representational structure. A key limitation of our multilingual setup is that the low-resource regime is represented by only two languages (Arabic and Hausa), so claims about low-resource temporal reasoning should be interpreted as suggestive rather than fully general across low-resource languages. More broadly, the language split into high-resource versus low-resource is necessarily coarse, and additional languages are needed to test whether the same bottleneck pattern holds across other typological profiles, scripts, and calendar traditions.
