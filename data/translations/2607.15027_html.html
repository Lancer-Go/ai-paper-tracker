摘要。Abstract.

人工智能驱动的系统在创意工作中的整合引发了艺术家和法律界关于所有权概念的争论。 然而，当人类和人工智能的贡献交织在一起时，对于如何定义和归属所有权，仍缺乏共识。 为了引发对这些紧张关系的批判性反思，我们设计了 ArtSplit，这是一个原型，可以明确量化人类和人工智能在创造性工作的不同阶段的贡献。该原型不是旨在解决所有权问题，而是用于引发艺术家对通过创意工作流程中可衡量的行动来归属所有权这一想法的反应。 我们认为，量化无法与艺术家对创作意图和代理的理解相一致，并且衡量所有权的努力可能会削弱艺术家理解和实践创造性工作的长期假设。这种批评挑战了将历史和社会关系转变为技术问题的冲动。The integration of AI-driven systems in creative work has sparked debates among artists and legal communities about notions of ownership. Yet there remains little consensus on how ownership should be defined and attributed when human and AI contributions are intertwined. To provoke critical reflection on these tensions, we designed ArtSplit, a provotype that explicitly quantifies human and AI contributions across different stages of creative work. Rather than aiming to resolve ownership, the provotype was used to elicit artists’ responses to the idea of attributing ownership through measurable actions in the creative workflow. We argue that quantification fails to align with artists’ understandings of creative intent and agency, and that efforts to measure ownership risk diluting long-standing assumptions through which artists understand and practice creative work. This critique challenges the impulse to transform a historically and socially situated relation into a technical problem.

1. 简介Introduction

Yet, around 2022, the use of AI in art began sparking intense controversies. Two things happened that year: AI image-generators such as Stable Diffusion222https://stability.ai/, 中途, Mid-journey333https://www.midjourney.com/home，和达尔-E, and Dall-E444https://openai.com/index/dall-e/供公众使用，这使得艺术作品中的人工智能变得非常普遍，不容忽视。其次，Jason M. Allen 使用人工智能生成的图像赢得了著名的艺术奖were released for public use, which made AI in artistic work too prevalent to ignore. And second, Jason M. Allen won a prestigious art award using an AI-generated image555https://edition.cnn.com/2022/09/03/tech/ai-art-fair-winner-controversy。艺术家们对他们的作品被用来训练这些系统的可能性表示愤怒. Artists have expressed anger about the possibility of their work being used to train these systems666https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2023/jan/23/its-the-opposite-of-art-why-illustrators-are-furious-about-ai。许多人回忆起吉卜力工作室的创始人在看过人工智能动画后说的“这感觉像是对生命本身的侮辱”. Many recalled Studio Ghibli’s founder saying after seeing an AI animation that ”it feels like an insult to life itself”777https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/hayao-miyazaki-on-ai-utterly-disgusted/。虽然对人工智能艺术的批评来自多个角度，但最常见的问题之一是：. While the critiques of AI art come from many angles, one of the most commonly raised questions is:用人工智能生成艺术作品的艺术家可以被视为作品的所有者吗？Can the artist who generated their artwork with AI be considered the owner of the work?

自动化部分或整个工作流程并不是什么新鲜事。此外，艺术工作作为一种劳动形式，与“普通”工作有许多共同的基本特征：工人，这里的艺术家，通常通过大量的努力，生产出其他人可以消费的东西，在这种情况下是观众；当产品被认为能够满足消费者的某些需求时，艺术家将直接由作为雇主的机构或同时也是消费者的专员获得报酬，或者在艺术家的能力得到高度认可的情况下，通过预付款的方式获得报酬。如果生产消费者可以享受的东西才是最重要的，那么在艺术作品中使用人工智能应该不会引起特殊问题——至少，与在其他形式的劳动中使用人工智能所引发的问题没有什么不同。 然而，许多人不愿意承认艺术作品允许任何形式的自动化。原因是艺术作品通常被认为与其他类型的作品不同，因为艺术家与艺术品有着独特的个人关系。 当订购的蛋糕送达时，面包师不再受其束缚（除非污染或面包师是名人等情况）。我们通常也不关心是否涉及自动化。相比之下，艺术作品即使在到达观众面前后仍然继续归功于艺术家。 艺术家通常会在作品上签名，或者无论如何，在某处作为作品的创作者而被认为是该作品的作者。这种关系称为作者身份：成为艺术品所有者的一种特殊方式Automating parts or entire processes of work is nothing new. Moreover, artistic work, as a form of labor, shares many basic characteristics with ’ordinary’ work: the worker, here the artist, produces, usually through substantial effort, something that others can consume, in this case the audience; when the product is deemed capable of satisfying some desires of consumers, the artist is paid, either directly by institutions acting as employers or by the commissioner who is also the consumer, or in cases where the artist’s capacities are highly recognized, through an advance payment. If producing something consumers can enjoy is all that matters, then using AI in artistic work should not cause special issues — at least, no different from those raised by using AI in other forms of labor. However, many are unwilling to grant that artistic work permits any form of automation. The reason is that artistic work is often perceived as different from other kinds of work, as the artist bears a unique and personal relation to the artwork. When an ordered cake is delivered, the baker is no longer tied to it (except in cases like contamination or if the baker is a celebrity). We also do not usually care if automation was involved. By contrast, artistic work continues to credit the artist even after it reaches the audience. The artist typically signs the work or, at any rate, is credited for the work somewhere as its creator. This relation is called authorship: a special way of being the owner of the artwork(Irvin, 2005)。即使艺术家既不拥有版权，也不拥有实物或数字代币，他们也是其艺术品的作者，因为艺术品是他们创造力的结果。正是因为这个想法，人工智能在艺术作品中的使用引起了争议——. The artist is the author of their artwork even when they own neither copyright nor the physical object or digital token, because the artwork has resulted from their creativity. And it is because of this idea that the use of AI in artistic work is controversial –如果人工智能系统帮助生成图像，艺术家还能被视为作者吗？Can the artist still be considered the author if an AI system helped generate the image?

人工智能艺术的反对者不愿意将艺术家视为作者的两个常见原因是：首先，是机器而不是人类艺术家进行“生成”；其次，其他艺术家提供的数据经常被用来输入机器Two common reasons opponents of AI art are unwilling to see artists as authors are: first, it is the machine, instead of the human artist, that does the “generating”; and second, the data provided by other artists is often used to feed the machine(Jiang et al., 2023)。第一个原因，可以说艺术家在创作过程中肯定做了一些事情——至少创造了提示并进行了迭代完善. For the first reason, one could say that the artist certainly has done something during the creative process - at the very least, has created prompts and made iterative refinements(Zeng et al., 2024)。有人可能怀疑，这里真正的问题是机器所做的工作不够透明，以至于我们无法评估它是否比艺术家所做的工作更有价值. The real issue here, one may suspect, is that the work done by the machine is not transparent enough for us to assess whether it is of more worth compared to the work done by the artist(Xu et al., 2024)。对于第二个问题，人们仍然会质疑一个典型的艺术创作的好案例是否不需要别人的参与。这里最重要的问题是，在人工智能介导的创意作品中，什么样的贡献被理解为保证所有权，以及是否存在一个门槛，在这个门槛上艺术家的参与被认为足以值得作者身份或信誉。. For the second issue, one could still question whether a typical good case of artistic creation involves no input from others. The overarching question here is in AI-mediated creative work, what kinds of contributions are understood to warrant ownership, and whether there exists a threshold at which an artist’s involvement is considered sufficient to merit authorship or credit.

在更传统的创意工作模式中，艺术家和艺术品之间的关系通过版权等法律框架得到阐明和稳定，该法律框架假定所有权源自人类创造者，其创造性决定可以追溯到最终作品。在这里（以及稍后的批评中），我们使用这个术语In more traditional models of creative work, the relation between artist and artwork has been articulated and stabilized through legal frameworks such as copyright, which presume that ownership originates in a human creator whose creative decisions can be traced to the final work. Here (and later in this critique), we use the term所有权ownership指一种智力或象征性的主张，通过这种主张，艺术家被认为是作品的创作者，包括通常所说的作者身份，例如创作起源、责任和身份的归属，而不是法律或物质所有权。to refer to an intellectual or symbolic claim through which an artist is recognized as the creator of a work, encompassing what is often described as authorship, such as the attribution of creative origin, responsibility, and identity, rather than legal or material ownership.

然而，随着生成式人工智能在艺术实践中的使用越来越多，这种假设变得越来越难以维持。法律和监管机构（尤其是在《欧盟人工智能法案》的背景下）With the growing use of generative AI in artistic practice, however, this presumption has become increasingly difficult to maintain. Legal and regulatory bodies (most notably in the context of the EU-AI Act(European Parliament and Council of the European Union, 2024)）已经开始重新审视当创造性工作是通过人类努力和自动化生成相结合而产生时，应如何理解所有权。这些讨论揭示的并不是一个明确的替代模型，而是现有模型的局限性：版权制度在很大程度上仍然面向单一的人类所有权，并且难以解释代理机构分布在人员、工具、数据和算法之间的创造性过程) have begun revisiting how ownership should be understood when creative work is produced through a combination of human effort and automated generation. What these discussions reveal is not a clear alternative model, but rather the limits of existing ones: copyright regimes remain largely oriented toward singular human ownership and struggle to account for creative processes in which agency is distributed across people, tools, data, and algorithms(Guadamuz, 2017; Irvin, 2005)。 作为回应，监管话语常常回归到诸如. In response, regulatory discourse often falls back on notions such as“人类的重大贡献”“significant human contribution”，表明所有权取决于一个人是否行使了足够的创造性控制或在作品上留下了可识别的个人印记, suggesting that ownership depends on whether a human has exercised sufficient creative control or left a recognizable personal imprint on the work(Xiao, 2023; Ramos-Zaga, 2025)。然而，这些标准仍然是定性的且不明确。对于如何在实践中比较、权衡甚至识别不同类型的参与（概念框架、提示制作、选择、迭代或后处理），它们几乎没有提供任何见解。这种模糊性在人工智能介导的创意工作中变得尤为突出，其中人类和机器的贡献随着时间的推移交织在一起，无法轻易分开。. Yet these criteria remain qualitative and loosely specified. They offer little insight into how different kinds of involvement—conceptual framing, prompt crafting, selection, iteration, or post-processing—should be compared, weighted, or even recognized in practice. This ambiguity becomes especially salient in AI-mediated creative work, where human and machine contributions are interwoven over time and cannot easily be separated.

这篇评论文章没有将这些模糊性视为需要解决的问题，而是将它们视为探究的场所。我们打算了解艺术家在使用人工智能时如何理解所有权，以及如何尝试使所有权可见，特别是通过定量或系统生成的表示，塑造他们对创意所有权的看法。为此，我们设计了一个人工智能驱动的原型Rather than treating these ambiguities as problems to be resolved, this critique paper approaches them as sites of inquiry. We intend to understand how artists make sense of ownership when working with AI, and how attempts to render ownership visible, particularly through quantitative or system-generated representations, shape their perceptions of creative ownership. To this end, we designed an AI-driven provotype(Boer and Donovan, 2012)故意以不同的方式运作所有权，不是作为最终的解决方案，而是作为挑衅。通过以这些原型的视频片段为支持的采访，我们探讨了这些框架如何肯定、挑战或动摇艺术家的主人翁意识。that deliberately operationalizes ownership in different ways, not as definitive solutions, but as provocations. Through interviews supported by video vignettes of these provotypes, we explore how such framings affirm, challenge, or destabilize artists’ sense of ownership.

2. 背景Background

长期以来，创造性工作一直被理解为在视觉艺术等领域产生新的、有意义的表达方式Creative work has long been understood as producing new and meaningful expressions across domains such as visual art(Gerber, 2020), 文学, literature(Doyle, 1998), 音乐, music(Cook, 2018), 设计, design(Bowen et al., 2016)，以及更多, and more(Brace and Johns-Putra, 2010; Hesmondhalgh and Baker, 2008; Goldsmith and Bridgstock, 2018)。超越原创性和审美价值. Beyond originality and aesthetic value(Boden, 2004; Runco and Jaeger, 2012)，创意实践受到更广泛的背景、文化和协作影响的影响, creative practice is shaped by broader contextual, cultural, and collaborative influences(Oldham and Cummings, 1996; Erez and Nouri, 2010; Abra, 1994)，并且与个人如何理解自己以及他们在世界上的地位密切相关。艺术家将绘画、写作、作曲或表演等活动描述为反思性实践，通过这些活动探索和表达自我意识, and is closely tied to how individuals understand themselves and their place in the world. Artists describe activities such as painting, writing, composing, or performing as reflective practices through which they explore and express their sense of self(Lingo and Tepper, 2013)。艺术家通过创造性的工作表达价值观. Through creative work, artists articulate values(Taylor and Littleton, 2016)、情绪, emotions(Radford, 2004)，以及世界观, and worldviews(Wilson, 2010)，塑造他们是谁以及他们代表什么的叙述, shaping narratives of who they are and what they stand for(Beech et al., 2016).

创造力与身份之间的关系常常与以下问题纠缠在一起：This relationship between creativity and identity is often entangled with questions of“所有权””ownership”和and“作者身份””authorship”。尽管这些术语经常互换使用，但它们具有不同的法律和概念含义. Although these terms are frequently used interchangeably, they carry distinct legal and conceptual meanings(Xu et al., 2024)。 所有权超越了法律认可，涵盖了个人依恋的主观意识（心理所有权），通过这种感觉，创作者将想法或制品体验为自己的. Ownership extends beyond legal recognition to encompass a subjective sense of personal attachment (psychological ownership), through which creators experience an idea or artifact as theirs(Wu et al., 2023; Pierce et al., 2003)。这种所有权感通常是通过参与创意过程以及使某些东西（无论是有形的还是无形的）存在的行为而形成的. This sense of ownership is frequently shaped through involvement in the creative process and the act of bringing something into existence, whether tangible or intangible(Baer and Brown, 2012)。 然而，在艺术实践中，作者身份仍然是一个更加流动和有争议的概念，其中经常缺乏正式的规则，并且作者身份的主张经常由艺术家自己主张，而不是由外部标准确定. Authorship, however, remains a more fluid and contested notion in artistic practice, where formalized rules are often absent, and claims to authorship are frequently asserted by artists themselves rather than determined by external criteria(Cronin, 2012)。 文化和社会心理学研究通过强调社会互动、沟通和协作在创造性工作中的作用，挑战个人主义的创造力观念. Research in cultural and social psychology challenges individualistic notions of creativity by emphasizing the role of social interaction, communication, and collaboration in creative work(Barrett et al., 2021; Sawyer and DeZutter, 2009)。创造性成果经常通过群体过程、共享认知和相互灵感而不是孤立的个人努力而出现. Creative outcomes frequently emerge through group processes, shared cognition, and mutual inspiration rather than isolated individual effort(Paulus, 2000)。在这种情况下，所有权和作者权之间的界限往往变得模糊，因为想法是通过共同创造和迭代输入共同开发的. In such contexts, the boundaries between ownership and authorship often blur as ideas are jointly developed through co-creation and iterative input(Dreyfuss, 2000)。然而，尽管存在这些合作现实，持久的文化叙事——例如孤独的艺术天才的形象——继续塑造围绕创意信用和认可的期望. Yet, despite these collaborative realities, enduring cultural narratives—such as the figure of the lone artistic genius—continue to shape expectations around creative credit and recognition(Cronin, 2012).

随着人工智能驱动的系统越来越多地融入创意实践，创造力、所有权和贡献的问题变得更加难以解决As AI-driven systems become increasingly embedded in creative practices, questions of creativity, ownership, and contribution have become more difficult to settle(Anantrasirichai and Bull, 2022)。艺术家经常将人工智能生成的输出描述为令人惊讶的，有时超出了人类的直接意图，而人类创造者策划、完善和重新解释了这些输出. Artists often describe AI-generated outputs as surprising in ways that sometimes exceed direct human intention, which human creators curate, refine, and reinterpret(Shelby et al., 2024)。这种互动在人类和非人类参与者之间重新分配创造性主动性，扰乱了人们对艺术代理和控制的常见假设。此外，人工智能辅助生成已被证明可以减轻劳动密集型创造性任务的负担，并支持协作工作中的协调和共享理解. Such interactions redistribute creative initiative across human and non-human actors, unsettling familiar assumptions about artistic agency and control. Moreover, AI-assisted generation has been shown to reduce the burden of labor-intensive creative tasks and support coordination and shared understanding in collaborative work(Han et al., 2024)。然而，这些可供性尚未就如何理解或评价人工智能辅助艺术达成共识。. Yet these affordances have not led to consensus about how AI-assisted art should be understood or valued.

一个普遍的立场认为，人工智能应该被视为一种工具，如相机或其他创意媒介，艺术家可以通过它来追求自己的意图，而不是本身就是一个作者One common position holds that AI should be treated as a tool like a camera or other creative medium, through which artists pursue their intentions, rather than as an author in its own right(Hertzmann, 2018, 2020)。从这个角度来看，将作者身份归因于人工智能可能会掩盖所涉及的人类劳动并削弱艺术家的作用. From this view, attributing authorship to AI risks obscuring the human labor involved and diminishing the artist’s role(Grba, 2021, 2022)。其他人指出，当代基于人工智能的实践涉及大量的创造性工作，例如快速制定、迭代实验和策展决策，所有这些都以有意义的方式塑造结果. Others point out that contemporary AI-based practices involve substantial creative work such as prompt formulation, iterative experimentation, and curatorial decision-making, all of which shape the outcome in meaningful ways(Chang et al., 2023)。尽管如此，实证研究表明创作者和观众一直担心人工智能生成的作品缺乏意图、情感投入和传统上与人类创造力相关的体验品质. Still, empirical studies reveal persistent concerns among creators and audiences that AI-generated works lack intention, emotional investment, and experiential qualities traditionally associated with human creativity(Shelby et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2024b; Hwang, 2022).

这些分歧的背后是一个持续存在的归因问题：创造性成果在多大程度上可以归功于人类艺术家而不是人工智能驱动的系统Underlying these disagreements is a persistent question of attribution: To what extent can a creative outcome be credited to the human artist rather than the AI-driven system(Samuelson, 1985; Hertzmann, 2020; Eshraghian, 2020; Wu et al., 2023)？由于人工智能生成的作品经常被视为复杂算法的产物，而不是个人思想的产物? As AI-generated works are frequently framed as products of complex algorithms rather than individual minds(Oksanen et al., 2023)，创作者常常难以划定清晰的界限, creators often struggle to draw clear boundaries(Chang et al., 2023)。他们倾向于从概念框架、内容把关和最终决策权方面来推理所有权，而不是呼吁可衡量的贡献. Rather than appealing to measurable contributions, they tend to reason about ownership in terms of conceptual framing, content gatekeeping, and final decision-making authority(Chang et al., 2023; Hwang et al., 2025)。在这些情况下，心理所有权通常受到感知参与、信用期望和对侵权的担忧等因素的影响. Psychological ownership in these contexts is often shaped by factors such as perceived involvement, expectations of credit, and concerns about infringement(Xu et al., 2024)。与此同时，法律框架几乎没有为人类与人工智能的合作提供具体指导. Concurrently, legal frameworks offer little concrete guidance on human–AI collaboration(Zirpoli, 2023; Watiktinnakorn et al., 2023)，让作者身份问题通过个人判断而不是既定规则进行谈判。, leaving questions of authorship to be negotiated through personal judgment rather than established rules.

从法律角度来看，这些模糊性问题并不容易解决。在欧盟，版权保护取决于作品是否具有原创性，因为它反映了作者自己的智力创作From a legal perspective, these ambiguities are not easily resolved. In the European Union, copyright protection hinges on whether a work is original in the sense that it reflects the author’s own intellectual creation(European Parliamentary Research Service, 2025)。欧盟法院的判例法强调，当作者做出自由和创造性的选择时，原创性就会产生，这些选择在作品的最终形式中可见. Case law from the Court of Justice of the European Union emphasizes that originality arises when an author makes free and creative choices that are visible in the final form of the work(Ramos-Zaga, 2025)。因此，版权保护不仅仅取决于努力或贡献，而取决于人类的创造性判断能否在作品的表达中得到识别. Copyright protection, therefore, does not depend on effort or contribution alone, but on whether human creative judgment can be identified in the expression of the work(Bently et al., 2022)。然而，欧盟法律没有对作者身份提供明确的定义，而是通过理论和判例法来解释此类决定. However, EU-law offers no explicit definition of authorship, leaving such determinations to interpretation through doctrine and case law(Fritz, 2024)。这些原创性测试为通过协作或技术中介产生的创造性工作提供了有限的指导，特别是当人类贡献是间接的或难以在输出中追踪时. These originality tests provide limited guidance for creative work that emerges through collaboration or technological mediation, particularly when human contributions are indirect or difficult to trace in the output(Xiao, 2023; Abbott and Rothman, 2023)。虽然根据版权法，人工智能驱动的系统本身不能成为作者，但完全由人工智能生成的输出不受保护. While AI-driven systems themselves cannot be authors under copyright law, outputs generated entirely by AI are excluded from protection(3)。然而，当生成系统实质上塑造了作品的表达特征时，即使是通过提示或后处理的人类参与也可能无法达到原创性阈值. Yet, even human involvement through prompting or post-processing may fail to meet the originality threshold when generative systems substantially shape the expressive features of the work(Becker, 2024)。因此，某些形式的人类与人工智能的创造性合作可能完全不受版权保护。. Consequently, certain forms of human–AI creative collaboration may fall outside copyright protection altogether.

总而言之，这些讨论表明，欧盟版权法依赖于一组有限且往往模糊的指标来识别创造性贡献。此外，人们对艺术家本身如何推理人类与人工智能协作中所有权的分配仍然知之甚少，特别是当创造性工作跨越构思、输入选择、迭代细化和算法生成等阶段展开时。Taken together, these discussions suggest that EU-copyright law relies on a limited and often ambiguous set of indicators for identifying creative contribution. Furthermore, there remains little understanding of how artists themselves reason about the distribution of ownership in human–AI collaboration, particularly when creative work unfolds across stages such as ideation, input selection, iterative refinement, and algorithmic generation.

5.1. 结果Results

在我们的分析过程中，我们认识到学术艺术家（AA）和内容创作者（CC）对原型有非常独特的观点和方法。因此，我们根据参与者的职业对结果进行了分类。然而，这并不意味着结果能够代表整个职业。这主要是本文的一个组织方案。 对访谈的主题分析确定了五个关键主题：During our analysis, we recognized that academic artists (AAs) and content creators (CCs) had very distinct perspectives and approaches to the provotype. Therefore, we categorized the results according to the professions of the participants. However, this does not imply that the results are representative of the professions as a whole. 5.1.1. 艺术观念决定作者身份Artistic Concept Determines Authorship

从内容创作者的角度来看，作者身份是由艺术理念决定的。内容创建者运用隐喻来说明他们的观点。 CC1和CC2强调人工智能只是加速这一过程的工具。用户根据他们的概念最终决定制作什么图像、何时令人满意以及何时结束生成过程。因此，用户仍然是艺术品的正当所有者。 CC1 依赖于大型协作流程的比较，例如建立公司或制作电影：From the content creators’ perspectives, authorship is determined by artistic concept. Content creators applied metaphors to illustrate their point. CC1 and CC2 emphasized that AI is just a tool to speed up the process. The user decides ultimately what image to make, when it is satisfactory, and when the generative process is concluded, according to their concept. So the user is still the proper owner of the artwork. CC1 relied on comparisons to large collaborative processes such as building a company or making a film:“比如说杰夫·贝索斯或那些大公司……当[他]开始向朋友征求对他们公司的意见时，如果他们的朋友给了他们最好的见解，并不意味着这是[他们朋友的]。这是他的想法！””Say Jeff Bezos or those big corporates … When [he] began and asked for opinions [from] friends for their companies, if their friend gave them the best insight [that] doesn’t mean it’s [their friends’]. It was his idea!”然而，CCs 对于艺术家的概念或想法如何在与生成人工智能的交互中准确地传达存在分歧。 CC1 认为艺术家的想法只是输入的提示，而 CC2 则认为，除了提示之外，艺术家执行的每一个行为 - 所有调整、改进、选择艺术品以供机器使用，以及决定作品何时完成 - 都算作传达概念。However, the CCs disagreed on how exactly the artists’ concepts or ideas are conveyed in interaction with generative AIs. While CC1 believed that the artist’s idea is simply the entered prompt, CC2 argued that every act the artist performs, in addition to the prompt - all adjustments, refinements, selecting the artwork to feed the machine, and deciding when the work is complete - counts as conveying the concept.

学院派艺术家对于所有权的决定因素持有不同的观点。虽然 AA1 和 AA2 与 CC 有着相同的信念（所有权是由基本概念定义的），但 AA3 对此主张持批评态度。 AA3 使用工厂工人和业主的比喻质疑这种范式：The academic artists held divergent perspectives on what determines ownership. While AA1 and AA2 had the same belief as the CCs (that ownership is defined by the underlying concept), AA3 was critical of the claim. AA3 questioned this paradigm using the metaphor of factory workers and owners:“工人们的双手长满了老茧，整天都在那里。但工厂老板可以说，我拥有机器和空间，通过拥有这个概念，我就拥有了工作。你把自己置于一个棘手的道德境地。””Workers are the ones who have calloused hands and are there all day. But factory owner could say, I own the machines and space, and by owning the concept, I own the work. You’re putting yourself in a tricky moral position.”

AA2 还质疑分配艺术所有权何时很重要：AA2 also questioned when assigning artistic ownership is important:“[假设]我的文章需要一只青蛙。现在，无论是在提示中还是改变风格或做其他事情，这是否算作某种劳动或创造性投入？我不知道这个百分比是否有帮助”。”[Say] I need a frog for my article. Now, whether it’s in the prompt or changing the style or doing something else, would [that] count as some sort of labor or creative input? I don’t know [if] the percentage helps”.对他们来说，当人工智能被用来生成用于功能目的的东西时，并不清楚它在计算作者身份方面完成了什么。 AA2 认为艺术家的概念超出了操作机器的行为，因为“To them, when AI is used to generate something for functional purposes, it is not clear what it accomplished to calculate authorship. AA2 suggested that the artist’s concept is something beyond the acts performed to operate the machine because ”提示的作用是给出一个比较笼统的指令”[what] the prompt does is giving an instruction that is somewhat general”，而这个概念与提示或任何其他可感知的行为并不相同。更重要的是，他们认为概念和指令是不同的东西：给出指令并不总是涉及传达背后更深层的意图或想法，传达概念通常并不采取给出一系列指令的形式。, while the concept is not identical to prompting, or any other perceivable act. More importantly, they believed that concept and instruction are distinct things: giving instructions does not always involve conveying a deeper intention or idea behind, and conveying a concept usually does not take the form of giving a series of instructions.

综上所述，大多数与会者强调了基本概念在确定所有权方面的作用，AA3 表达了不同的观点；虽然大多数人将所有权归于概念提供者，但 AA1 还建议，其数据用于训练图像生成器的贡献者可以获得部分所有权（约 25-30%）。In summary, most participants emphasized the role of the underlying concept in determining ownership, with AA3 expressing a divergent view; while the majority attributed ownership to the concept provider, AA1 also suggested that contributors whose data was used to train the image-generator could merit a partial share of ownership (approximately 25–30%).

5.1.2. 所有权的量化Quantifiability of Ownership

在观看视频原型后，我们询问参与者是否认为所有权分配公平。After being shown the video provotype, we asked participants if they found the distributions of ownership fair.

内容创作者都认为应该给予艺术家更多的数字。 CC1 表示“The content creators both thought that the artist overall should be given a greater number. CC1 said that ”如果艺术家自己的作品被纳入其中，他应该至少获得70%。if the artist’s own work is being fed into it, he should get at least 70%.CC2 认为，在所有变体中，细化应该得到更多重视，而提示和草图则被高估了。然而，他们的语气不确定，立场摇摆不定，解释也基于直觉。在某些情况下，他们通过场景之间的比较形成了一种公平感。例如，他们发现艺术家在面向输入的变体和面向输入+细化的变体中获得类似的分布是“不公平的”，因为艺术家在后者中完成了更多的工作。其他时候，他们的推理是基于反应性的感受。例如，CC1 表示使用面向输入变体的艺术家是” CC2 thought that refinement ought to receive more weight in all variants, while prompting and sketches were overrated. However, their tones were uncertain, positions were shifty, and explanations were based on intuition. In certain cases, they formed a sense of fairness based on the comparisons among the scenarios. For example, they found it ”unfair” that the artist got a similar distribution in the Input-Oriented Variant and the Input+Refinement-Oriented Variant because the artist performed more work in the latter. Other times, their reasoning was based on reactive feelings. For example, CC1 expressed that the artist using the Input-Oriented Variant was“懒惰””being lazy”, 和, and“利用人工智能，让人工智能完成所有工作””taking advantage of AI, while AI does all the work”。当面对他们之前的主张时（这个概念决定所有权），内容创作者以两种不同的方式理解他们的经历。 CC1 将人工智能更多地视为合作者而不是工具，并相信从某种意义上说，人工智能可以拥有所制作艺术品的部分所有权。 CC2表示，原型追踪的可能不是所有权，而是贡献，这与所有权不同。他们指出，原型以“公平”的方式计算贡献，只要数字反映了实际完成的工作。. When confronted with their earlier claims — that concept determines ownership — the content creators made sense of their experiences in two different ways. CC1 had come to view AI more as a collaborator than a tool and believed it could, in some sense, hold a share of ownership in the artwork produced. And CC2 suggested that what the prototype tracked may not be ownership, but rather contribution, which is distinct from ownership. They noted that the prototype calculated the contribution in a “fair” way, as long as the numbers reflected the actual work done.

所有学术艺术家都质疑所有权分配的公平性意味着什么。他们的回答指出了对创作过程中所有权或贡献的量化的更广泛的担忧。 AA1 质疑是否有可能量化真正随机过程完成的工作量：”All academic artists questioned what it meant for an assignment of ownership to be fair. Their responses pointed to broader concerns about the quantifiability of ownership, or contribution, in the creative process. AA1 questioned whether it was even possible to quantify the amount of work done by genuinely random processes: ”100% 随机的事物（获得几乎任何事物的机会均等）能否公平？Can there be fairness in something that is 100% random, where there is an equal chance of getting just about anything?”AA1 用掷骰子的比喻解释说” Using the metaphor of rolling a die, AA1 explained that“有时需要多次滚动才能获得正确的图像，有时您只是碰巧立即滚动所需的数量！””sometimes it takes multiple rolls to get the right image, and sometimes you just happen to roll the desired number right away!”在前一种情况下，机器可能看起来做了更多的工作，但这并不是因为它更有效率或更勤奋——相反，机会只是对用户不利。目前尚不清楚在这种情况下公平评估人工智能的贡献意味着什么。In the former case, the machine may appear to have done more work, but not because it is more productive or diligent — rather, chance is simply working against the user. It remains unclear what it would mean to fairly assess the AI’s contribution under such conditions.

关于视频片段，AA1 指出，他们没有获得有关输入机器的图像的足够信息：About the video vignettes, AA1 pointed out that they were not given enough information about the images fed into the machine:“为了评估 ArtSplit 是否公平地计算了它进行了多少‘数据处理’，我们需要知道输入了什么样的图像，它们是否与期望的结果显着不同。只有这样，我们才能评估 ArtSplit 是否生成了与输入的图像显着不同的图像，或者只是选择了与输出非常相似的图像并仅做了一些小的更改。了解这一点对于评估它进行了多少‘数据处理’是必要的。””To assess whether ArtSplit has fairly calculated how much ’data processing’ it did, we need to know what kind of images were fed into it, whether they are significantly different from the desired result. Only then could we assess whether ArtSplit has generated an image significantly different from the images fed into it or has simply picked something very similar to the output and merely done some small alterations. Knowing this is necessary for assessing how much ’data processing’ it did.”

AA2 质疑是否可以有一个确定的方法来衡量贡献：AA2 questioned whether there can be a definitive way to weigh the contribution:“有什么理由将激励占到工作的 20%，而不是 30%？””What kind of justification could there be for giving prompting as 20% of the work instead of 30%?”换句话说，写提示的工作怎么能与机器处理数据等工作相提并论呢？ AA2 建议贡献的权重可以定制——用户应该能够设置提示的数量。然而，AA2 仍然坚持认为，计算机无法充分跟踪创作过程中的许多重要元素，尤其是在捕捉概念时，正如上一个主题中所讨论的那样。In other words, how could the work of writing the prompt be commensurable with, for example, data processing done by the machine? AA2 suggested that the weighing of the contribution could be made customizable - the user should be able to set how much the prompt would count. However, AA2 still insisted that many important elements of the creative process cannot be adequately tracked by the computer, especially when it comes to capturing the concept, as discussed in the previous theme.

当我们询问 AA3 创作过程中的哪些步骤属于作者身份时，AA3 进一步提出了这个问题：AA3 pushed the question further when we asked them which steps in the creative process authorship lies:“你必须假设创作过程中有多个步骤。你必须假设作者身份位于特定的步骤中，而不是整体存在于其中。””You must assume that there are steps in the creative process. And you must assume that authorship sits in specific steps rather than sort of an overall presence in it.”AA3解释说，在艺术品完成之前，“概念”永远不会停止发展；每一个新的举动都可能导致艺术家重新审视他们的概念。对他们来说，艺术过程更像是一个多节点反馈循环，而不是具有单独的预定步骤的东西。AA3 explained that the ’concept’ never ceases to develop until the artwork is completed; each new move could lead the artist to review their concept. To them, the artistic process is more like a multi-nodal feedback loop than something with separate predetermined steps.

总体而言，内容创作者倾向于接受所有权是可量化的，并倾向于将更大的所有权分配给艺术家，通常通过直觉和跨场景比较进行推理，而学院派艺术家仍然对量化深表怀疑，他们的批评基于人工智能图像生成的技术知识和对创作过程的理解。Overall, the content creators tended to accept ownership as quantifiable and favored assigning greater ownership to the artist, often reasoning through intuition and comparisons across scenarios, whereas the academic artists remained deeply skeptical of quantification, grounding their critiques in both the technical knowledge of AI image-generation and understandings of the creative process.

5.1.3. 拨款Appropriation

无论参与者是否相信 ArtSplit 按预期追踪所有权，他们都设想了它可能会有所帮助的方式。对于内容创作者来说，ArtSplit 可以起到激励作用，即使他们建议将其视为贡献而不是所有权的跟踪器。 CC1 表示，这会激励他们不要“懒惰”，只需输入一个又一个提示，然后让机器完成工作即可。 CC1 知道机器对产品具有可衡量的贡献，因此认为包含一些可以使工作成为自己的东西变得更加重要。例如，他们提到他们将确​​保在最终产品中包含他们的艺术签名。 CC2 还提到，ArtSplit 可以让他们更加了解创作过程以及其中的不同类型的工作，无论是来自人类还是机器。它将鼓励他们更深入地思考在艺术中使用人工智能的影响。Whether or not participants believed that ArtSplit tracked ownership as intended, they envisioned ways it could be helpful. For content creators, ArtSplit could serve motivational purposes, even when they suggested that it should be seen as a tracker for contribution instead of ownership. CC1 said that it would motivate them to not be ’lazy’, simply entering prompt after prompt and letting the machine do the work. Knowing that the machine has a measurable contribution to the product, CC1 thought it had become more important to include something that would make the work their own. For example, they mentioned that they would make sure to include their artistic signature in the final product. CC2 also mentioned that ArtSplit could make them more aware of the creative process and the different kinds of work that went into it, be it from the human or the machine. It would encourage them to reflect more deeply on the implications of using AI in art.

学院派艺术家在与 ArtSplit 互动时往往更加慎重。 AA1 接受了使用它作为对其进行一定程度的叙述或批评的方式的想法。Academic artists tended to be more deliberate in interacting with ArtSplit. AA1 entertained the idea of using it as a way of providing a degree of narrative or critique to it.“我怎样才能真正推动数字走向相反的方向，使所有权（个人的所有权）最小化？我们已经接受了这个想法，并且我们已经将其完全抽象到机器可以达到的程度，同时仍然必须涉及概念、想法或批评！””How could I actually nudge the numbers to go the other way to the point where the ownership - the personal human ownership - is minimal? We’ve taken this idea and we’ve abstracted it entirely to as far as the machine can go while still having to be about the concept, the idea, or the critique!”AA2 认为 ArtSplit 对于跟踪差异很有用，这可能有助于避免版权冲突。例如，如果图像的更改超过 40%，则使用该图像并不侵犯版权，则可以使用 ArtSplit 来跟踪该差异。此外，在设计某些东西只是为了让它脱颖而出的情况下，例如一本书的封面，它可以再次用于跟踪特定设计与预先存在的设计相比有多么不同。他们还评论说，在这种情况下，作者身份不再重要：AA2 thought that ArtSplit would be useful for tracking differences that might help avoid copyright conflicts. For example, if the use of an image does not violate copyright if it is altered by more than 40%, then ArtSplit could be used to track that difference. Also, in the case of designing something solely to have it stand out, such as a cover for a book, it can again be used to track how different a particular design is compared to the pre-existing ones. They also commented that in such cases, authorship no longer mattered:“它达到了目的，但我认为不一定有任何对作者身份的渴望……我认为这更多的是实现某种功能的渴望。””It does the purpose, but I don’t think there’s any desire necessarily [for] authorship … I think it’s more [of] a desire to fulfill a function.”

总体而言，CC 认为 ArtSplit 对于激励和理解目的很有用，表明可能信任该系统提供有意义的所有权评估的潜力。相反，AA 对 ArtSplit 实现其预期功能的能力更加怀疑，并提出了自己的使用方式。Overall, the CCs believed that ArtSplit would be useful for motivation and comprehension purposes, indicating possible trust in the system’s potential for providing meaningful assessment of ownership. On the contrary, the AAs were more skeptical of ArtSplit’s ability to fulfill its intended function and came up with their own ways of using it.

5.1.4. 可控性Controllability

讨论中的可控性主要体现在两个方面：一是ArtSplit目前的设计能否给艺术家在创作过程中提供控制感；其次，ArtSplit 本身是否可以设计为提供更大的控制。 在内容创作者中，CC2 提到他们希望在自己的作品中使用 ArtSplit，因为没有它，他们感到缺乏控制：”Controllability emerged in the discussions in two ways: first, whether ArtSplit’s current design could offer artists a sense of control during the creative process; and second, whether ArtSplit itself could be designed to afford greater control. Among the content creators, CC2 mentioned that they would like to use ArtSplit in their own work because without it, they felt a lack of control: ”我不想感觉 [...] 图像生成工具超出了我的能力范围。”I don’t want to feel like […] the image-generation tool is beyond me.”它让他们得以一睹图像生成黑匣子内发生的事情。他们相信这会给他们在创作过程中带来自信和稳定的感觉。It offered them a glimpse into what was happening inside the blackbox of image-generation. They believed it would give them a sense of confidence and stability during the creative process.

学院派艺术家对 ArtSplit 提供的可控性更加怀疑。 AA3 质疑 ArtSplit 是否会给用户一种错误的控制感：虽然贡献的细分允许用户看到他们所做的事情，但测量的类别仅限于 ArtSplit 能够跟踪的类别。随着时间的推移，艺术家有动力采取的创造性行动可能会受到系统可衡量的限制，从而无意中缩小了创造性过程中使用的方法范围。 AA3 还建议，在整个过程中，应该让用户可以看到细分情况，就像字数计数器如何可以明显地跟踪键入时所写的字数一样。这将使艺术家实时了解已经完成了哪些工作以及完成了多少。The academic artists were more skeptical about the controllability provided by ArtSplit. AA3 questioned whether ArtSplit might give users a false sense of control: while the breakdown of contributions allows users to see what they have done, the categories measured are limited to those that ArtSplit is able to track. Over time, the creative actions that artists are motivated to take could become limited to those measurable by the system, unintentionally narrowing the range of methods used in the creative process. AA3 also suggested that the breakdown should be made visible to the user throughout the process, like how a word-counter can visibly track the number of words written as they are being typed. That would give the artist real-time awareness of what work has been done and how much.

5.1.5. 不正当的意图和邪恶的案件Perverse Intention & Wicked Case

有两种情况会破坏 ArtSplit 的目的。首先，数字本身成为激励，其次，数字失去了意义。There are two situations in which the purpose of ArtSplit is subverted. First, the numbers themselves become the incentive, and second, the numbers lose meaning.

我们创建 ArtSplit 的假设是艺术家希望他们的作者身份（或所有权）意识得到肯定。我们还假设作者身份（或所有权）与创作过程中所执行的行为成正比（灵感来自We created ArtSplit under the assumption that artists would like to have their sense of authorship (or ownership) affirmed. We also assumed that authorship (or ownership) is proportional to the acts performed during the creative process (inspired by(Xu et al., 2024; Hwang et al., 2025)）。在这种假设下进行设计的结果是，可以通过简单地执行更多操作来操纵数字，即使这些操作本身并不理想。当艺术家为了获得更高的贡献而采取行动时，创作过程就会脱轨，不再具有真正的艺术性。). The consequence of designing under such an assumption is that the numbers could be manipulated by simply performing more actions, even when these actions are not desirable in themselves. The creative process is derailed and ceases to be genuinely artistic when the artist is incentivized to perform actions just to get a higher number for their contribution.

当艺术家获得完全符合他们期望的图像时，就会出现相反的情况，即数字对艺术家不再有意义。鉴于人工智能图像生成过程中始终存在一定程度的随机性，这种情况可能会在很早的时候发生，当时艺术家所做的工作很少，因此做出的贡献也相对较小。当被问及他们是否会继续对图像进行工作只是为了获得更高的数字时，所有人都说他们不会，并且他们不会关心这个数字：The opposite scenario, where the numbers are no longer meaningful to the artist, occurs when the artist obtains an image that meets their expectation exactly. Given that there is always a degree of randomness in the process of AI image-generation, this could happen very early on, when the artist has done very little work and hence made relatively little contribution. When asked whether they would continue to work on the image simply to get a higher number, all said that they would not, and that they would not care about the number:“这不是我获得 55% 的功劳，而是获得我想要的艺术品””It’s not about me getting 55% credit but getting the artwork I wanted”（CC1）。(CC1).

这表明所有参与者——学者和内容创作者——都有一种不同的所有权范式的潜在意识，其中作者身份不是由完成的工作量或传达的概念决定的，而是由作者在最终图像中看到自己的愿景的程度决定的。This suggests that all participants - academics and content creators - had an underlying sense of a different paradigm of ownership, where authorship is not determined by the amount of work done, or the concept conveyed, but instead by how much the author can see their own vision in the resulting image.

6. 讨论Discussion

在这篇评论文章中，我们旨在探讨在人类与人工智能创意合作中沟通所有权崩溃的影响，重点是艺术家如何感知和理解所有权。基于两次初步采访，我们开发了一个名为 ArtSplit 的原型，它通过三种变体传达共同生成图像的所有权：面向细化、面向输入和面向输入+细化。然后，我们对五位艺术家进行了半结构化采访，观察他们对这些不同故障的反应，并深入了解他们对所有权（或作者身份）的理解以及影响他们看法的因素。下面，我们讨论我们的结果。In this critique paper, we aimed to explore the implications of communicating ownership breakdown in human–AI creative collaboration, with a focus on how artists perceive and understand ownership. Based on two preliminary interviews, we developed a provotype called ArtSplit that communicated ownership over a co-generated image via three variants: refinement-oriented, input-oriented, and input+refinement-oriented. We then conducted semi-structured interviews with five artists to observe their reactions to these different breakdowns and gain insight into their understandings of ownership (or authorship) and the factors that shape their perceptions of it. In the following, we discuss our results.

6.1. 贡献与贡献作者身份Contribution v.s. Authorship

ArtSplit 设计中的一个隐含假设是作者身份根据贡献分配。然而，我们的参与者认为只有某些类型的贡献与作者身份相关，特别是那些涉及概念交流和确保结果符合艺术家意图所需的工作的贡献。 这种看法的基础可能在于这样一个事实：一件艺术品既是一件艺术品，又是一个物体。尽管使艺术品成为一个物体需要不同种类的工作，但并非所有工作都有助于其艺术价值。因此，ArtSplit 实际追踪的内容并不被视为作者身份，而是被视为贡献。An implicit assumption that went into the design of ArtSplit was that authorship is distributed according to contribution. However, our participants considered only certain types of contributions to be relevant to authorship, particularly those involving the communication of concepts and the work required to ensure that the results aligned with the artist’s intent. The basis for this perception likely rests on the fact that an artwork is at once a piece of art and an object. Although different kinds of work are necessary to bring the artwork into being as an object, not all contribute to its artistic value. Hence, what ArtSplit actually tracks was not perceived as authorship but as contribution.

自文艺复兴以来，协作艺术实践就已存在Collaborative artistic practices have existed since the Renaissance(Wallace, 1989; Horne et al., 1995)。今天的艺术家仍然雇用助手来完成艺术创作和设置复杂装置的繁重工作。的. Artists today still employ assistants to do the grunt work in artistic production and setting up complex installations. The作者Auteur电影研究理论将导演视为主要创作者，或者说Theory in Film Studies poses the director as the main creator, or the作者auteur（法语中“作者”的意思）的制作，尽管电影制作往往需要密切合作(French word for author) of the production, although filmmaking tends to be intensely collaborative(Santas, 2002)。此外，现代艺术中的运动以及实现这一点的技术（尤其是 AR 和 VR）催生了互动艺术：一种只有在观众参与下才能实现的艺术品。在这种情况下，观众自己就成为了合作者，尽管他们常常不知道艺术家的意图或期望是什么。在这些例子中，我们不倾向于争论作者是谁。因此，要声称与人工智能的协作会导致所有权方面的特殊问题，我们必须阐明人工智能与其他人类协作者的具体区别。. Furthermore, movements within modern art alongside the technologies enabling this (especially AR and VR) gave rise to interactive art: a kind of artwork that only becomes actualized with viewers’ participation. In such cases, viewers themselves become collaborators, though often they are ignorant of the artist’s intention or what to expect. In these examples, we do not tend to dispute who the author is. Thus, to claim that collaboration with AI causes special problems for ownership, we must articulate how exactly AI differs from other human collaborators.

6.2. 追踪艺术进程Tracking Artistic Processes

参与者对“艺术观念”的不同理解，促使我们质疑追踪艺术行为在创作实践中是否必要甚至可行。 首先，如果概念决定所有权，那么，在这种理解下，任何不符合“传达概念”行为的作品都可能不被视为相关或计入所有权。 其次，即使我们将这种细分解释为跟踪贡献而不是所有权，关于哪些行为应该属于“传达艺术概念”的类别仍然存在问题——这是否只包括提示或艺术家执行的所有行为。 第三，如果 ArtSplit 跟踪的行为没有一个符合传达概念的条件——正如 AA2 所建议的，他将提示比作给出指令而不是表达概念——那么系统可能会省略一个不可或缺的组件。 这表明人工智能驱动的系统在捕捉创意过程的某些方面可能面临挑战。The participants’ diverging understandings of “artistic concept” prompted us to question whether tracking artistic acts is necessary or even feasible in creative practice. First, if concept determines ownership, then, within this understanding, any work that does not qualify as an act of “conveying a concept” may not be considered relevant or counted towards ownership. Second, even if we interpret the breakdown as tracking contribution rather than ownership, questions remain regarding which acts should fall under the category of “conveying an artistic concept” - whether this includes only prompting or all actions performed by the artist. Third, if none of the acts tracked by ArtSplit qualify as conveying a concept — as suggested by AA2, who compared prompting to giving instructions rather than expressing concepts — then an indispensable component may be omitted by the system. This suggests that AI-driven systems may face challenges in capturing certain aspects of the creative process.

我们应该提出的另一个问题是量化创造性工作的组成部分意味着什么，因为参与者讨论的类别似乎以难以用计算工件捕获的方式深深地纠缠在一起。 当艺术家参与作品的其他部分时，这个概念可能会在背景中不断变化。然而，像 ArtSplit 这样的系统可以跟踪细化，但不能跟踪艺术家头脑中发生的事情。 当我们考虑到一个人的提示技巧随着一个人在撰写提示方面变得更加有经验而变得更好这一事实时，就出现了进一步的问题。 因此，经验丰富的艺术家可以通过更少的提示迭代来实现所需的结果。然而，根据参与者对贡献的理解，可观察到的行为减少并不一定意味着贡献或作者身份的减少。这与当代的格言产生了共鸣：Another question we should pose is what it means to quantify the components of creative work, given that the categories participants discussed appeared deeply entangled in ways that may be difficult to capture with computational artifacts. However, systems such as ArtSplit could track refinement, but not what is happening in the artist’s mind. A further question arises when we consider the fact that one’s prompting skill gets better as one becomes more experienced in writing prompts. Yet, within participants’ understandings of contribution, fewer observable acts would not necessarily imply reduced contribution or authorship. This resonates with the contemporary adage:你支付的不是几分钟的工作费用，而是为学习如何在几分钟内完成工作而花费的时间支付的费用you are not paying for a few minutes of work, but for the years it took to learn how to do it in a few minutes121212https://medium.com/@belikenikola/the-phrase-you-dont-pay-me-for-the-5-minutes-i-spent-to-do-it-you-have-to-pay-me-for-10-years-i-f7b3064d7284。 解决这些问题的一种方法是让艺术家根据自己对决定作者身份的理解，自由地决定哪些行为应该更重要。然而，这将带来进一步的挑战。一方面，当不允许艺术家设定自己的指标时，系统可能会产生与他们的理解相冲突的结果。另一方面，如果他们可以随意设置指标，那么这些数字可能会变得毫无意义，因为艺术家可能会简单地以始终将他们指定为主要作者的方式配置系统。. One way to address these problems would be to give artists the freedom to decide which acts should weigh more, according to their own understanding of what determines authorship. However, this would introduce further challenges. On one hand, when artists are not allowed to set their own metric, the system may yield results that conflict with their understanding. On the other hand, if they are free to set the metric however they like, then the numbers could become meaningless, as artists might simply configure the system in a way that always designates them as the primary author.

6.3. 当原创性很重要时When Originality Matters

我们设计原型所依赖的另一个前提是原创性很重要：因为艺术家可能想知道他们的创造力有多少融入到最终的艺术作品中，所以他们可能会对使用像我们这样的人工智能驱动系统感兴趣。然而，根据艺术品的目的，作者身份在某些情况下并不那么重要。正如 AA2 指出的那样，当作品用于满足某些功能（例如满足营销需求或避免版权冲突）时，通常不需要作者身份。这个问题可以被视为将所有权与贡献混为一谈的另一个后果：在某些情况下，用户可能主要寻求对其贡献的认可，而不需要确定是否有人应被视为该作品的作者。因此，坚持在每种情况下都确定作者是谁可能会产生难以回答的问题，因为这些问题不需要首先提出。Another presupposition we relied on for designing the provotype was that originality matters: it is because the artist may want to know how much of their creativity has gone into the resulting artwork that they may take an interest in using an AI-driven system like ours. However, depending on the purpose of the artwork, authorship matters less in some cases. As AA2 pointed out, authorship is usually not desired when the piece serves to fulfill certain functions, such as meeting marketing demands or avoiding copyright conflicts. This issue could be seen as another consequence of conflating ownership with contribution: in some contexts, users may primarily seek acknowledgment of their contribution, without needing to determine whether anyone should be considered the author of the piece. Insisting on deciding who the author is in every case can therefore give rise to questions that are difficult to answer because they need not be asked in the first place.

此外，跟踪贡献并不意味着跟踪原创性：工作正在完成的事实并不能说明其原创性。 AA1 表示，如果 ArtSplit 能够将生成的图像与互联网上的所有图像进行比较，那么它可能能够表明艺术品的原创性。然而，我们怀疑即使这样也还不够，因为原创性可能不仅仅意味着差异。想想杜尚的《L.H.O.O.Q.》，这是一张带有面部毛发的《蒙娜丽莎》明信片Furthermore, tracking contribution does not imply tracking originality: the fact that work is being done does not say anything about its originality. AA1 suggested that if ArtSplit were able to compare the resulting image with all images on the internet, then it might have been capable of indicating the originality of the artwork. However, we suspect that even that would not be enough, because originality may not mean mere differences. Consider Duchamp’s L.H.O.O.Q., a postcard of the Mona Lisa with facial hair131313https://www.nortonsimon.org/art/detail/P.1969.094。这幅画看起来几乎和《蒙娜丽莎》一模一样，但小胡子独特地体现了杜尚的艺术性格。 从这个角度来看，创作作为一种有意识的活动，其新颖性并不是因为它恰好不同于以前的任何创造，而是因为创作者独特的艺术理念. The image looks almost exactly like the Mona Lisa, but the little beard uniquely reflects Duchamp’s artistic character. From this perspective, creative work, being a kind of conscious activity, is not novel because it happens to be unlike anything previously created, but rather because of the unique artistic concept of the creator(Stein, 1953).

6.4. 理解数字Making Sense of The Numbers

根据 HCI 指南，良好的设计通常通过直观的比较来提供透明度和可解释性A good design, according to HCI guidelines, affords transparency and explainability, usually through enabling intuitive comparisons(Guo et al., 2024a; Oladele, 2024; Xu et al., 2024)。然而，我们质疑像 ArtSplit 这样的系统是否仅仅提供了一种透明的幻觉。 在我们的案例研究中，参与者的人工智能素养水平似乎决定了他们如何批评 ArtSplit 生成的数字。 学术艺术家们意识到所测量的数字和类别都是虚构的，并以此作为他们批评的起点。他们讨论了量化的可能性以及如何使这些数字引人注目或有用。与此同时，CC 假设我们知道自己在做什么，因此相信这些数字是有意义的并且贡献是可以量化的。 CC们没有理由质疑ArtSplit的可行性，只能对某些场景下的具体贡献分配表示不满。换句话说，学者们能够质疑贡献和作者身份的“公平”分配是否可能，而 CC 则认为这是可能的，并希望数字能够朝着证实他们直觉的方向推动。. However, we question whether systems like ArtSplit offer merely an illusion of transparency. In our case study, participants’ levels of AI literacy appeared to shape how they criticized the numbers generated by ArtSplit. The Academic Artists were aware that the numbers and categories being measured were made-up, and used that as the starting point for their critique. They discussed the possibilities of quantification and how the numbers could be made compelling or useful. Meanwhile, the CCs assumed we knew what we were doing and hence believed that the numbers were meaningful and contributions were quantifiable. The CCs did not have a basis for questioning the feasibility of ArtSplit, but could only express dismay about the specific assignments of contribution in certain scenarios. In other words, the academics were able to question whether a ”fair” assignment of contribution and authorship was even possible, whereas the CCs assumed that it was possible and hoped that the numbers could be nudged in a direction that affirmed their intuition.

我们打算通过提供贡献的数字细分来营造一种主人翁意识，并通过这种方式为用户提供更透明的艺术创作体验。但这种透明感可能是一种幻觉——只有当用户能够独立于这样的系统而知道如何衡量每一类贡献时，这些数字才可能真正有意义并令人信服（就像对于不懂算术运算的人来说，计算器的输出是神秘的）。We intended to create a sense of ownership by providing a numerical breakdown of contributions, and through that, offer the user a more transparent experience of artistic creation. But this sense of transparency could be an illusion–it is when the user is capable of knowing, independent of such a system, how each category of contribution ought to be measured, that the numbers could be truly meaningful and convincing to them (in the same way that, for someone who does not understand arithmetic operations, the outputs of a calculator are mysterious).

本节中提到的观察结果还引起了一个道德问题：是否允许测试似乎测量用户可能无法测量的事物的原型？在参与我们的研究之前，CC们从未考虑过艺术贡献是否可以衡量。但采访结束后，他们似乎相信这是可能的，因为我们冒充科学家向他们展示了这样一个原型。采访可能给了他们错误的信念。The observations noted in this section also give rise to an ethical issue: Is it permissible to test prototypes that appear to measure things that might not be measurable with users? Before taking part in our study, the CCs had never thought about whether artistic contribution could be measurable. But after the interviews, they seemed to believe that it is possible to do so, just because we showed them such a prototype while posing as scientists. The interviews may have given them false beliefs.

6.5. 设计少？Design Less?

我们得出的最后一点是：是否真的需要像 ArtSplit 这样的人工智能驱动系统？它解决的是实际问题，还是仅仅是更深层次结构性问题的症状？批评技术解决主义的文献指出，案例研究的含义是“不是设计（技术）”The final point we arrive at is: Is an AI-driven system such as ArtSplit actually needed? Does it address an actual problem, or merely the symptoms of deeper structural issues? Literature critiquing technosolutionism has noted that the implication of case studies is ”not to design (technology)”(Bødker et al., 2017)。当技术不是最佳解决方案时进行设计可能会以意想不到的方式改变问题，或者引导研究人员解决问题. Designing when technology is not the best solution could transform the issue in unanticipated ways, or lead researchers to solve“问题的计算可追踪转换，而不是问题本身””a computationally trackable transformation of a problem rather than the problem itself” (Baumer and Silberman, 2011).

就我们而言，还值得注意的是，当代正式“拥有”艺术品的需求是由我们当前的社会历史背景决定的。 现代版权概念是在大量印刷技术发明之后出现的，该技术使可复制的作品能够被买卖In our case, it is also worth noting that the contemporary need to formally ”own” the artwork is shaped by our current social-historical context. Fyre(Frye, 2020)认为艺术家和学者需要将他们的创作或想法归因于他们，这是根据艺术品和出版物的生产分配权力的结果。 这就提出了一个问题：在这个复杂的问题网络中引入量化是否是一个适当的应对措施。argues that the need among artists and academics to have their creations or ideas attributed to them is a result of power being distributed according to the production of artwork and publications. This raises the question of whether introducing quantification into this complex web of problems is an appropriate response.

By confronting academic artists and content creators with a provotype that explicitly quantified human and AI contributions, we sought to elicit reflective discussion and to better understand how creative workers themselves perceive artistic ownership in such collaborations. There are two primary limitations to our study. The first concerns the number of participants, which is smaller than what is expected in HCI research. Our analysis was informed by Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis (IPA), a methodology that values the subjective and situated experiences of individuals(Smith et al., 2021)。因此，我们优先考虑丰富、细致的描述而不是普遍性。与其他定性方法一致. The small sample size is further justified by the depth of engagement required and the logistical complexity inherent in conducting such qualitative research.

Consequently, key contextual factors may have been overlooked, and participants’ immediate reactions—especially during time-limited interviews—may not reflect their deeper or evolving perspectives. Nevertheless, despite the aforementioned limitations, this methodology enabled the possibility to confront participants with a not-yet-existing practice.

总而言之，这项工作并没有解决人工智能辅助创造力的所有权问题，而是动摇了此类问题应首先通过系统设计来解决的假设。通过使所有权明确（并且可测量），像 ArtSplit 表面张力这样的系统超越了界面或度量选择，而是指向关于作者身份、价值和创造性劳动的历史上偶然的假设。从这个意义上说，我们的原型并不是主张更好的量化所有权的方法，而是引发人们反思量化是否有风险具体化和操作化担忧，而这些担忧本身就是更广泛的社会、法律和制度安排的产物。因此，我们将这项工作定位为对完善所有权跟踪系统的呼吁，而是对通过计算解决可能完全抵制技术解决方案的问题的冲动的批判。Taken together, this work does not so much resolve questions of ownership in AI-assisted creativity as it unsettles the assumption that such questions should be addressed through system design in the first place. By making ownership explicit (and measurable), systems like ArtSplit surface tensions that extend beyond interface or metric choice, pointing instead to historically contingent assumptions about authorship, value, and creative labor. In this sense, our provotype does not argue for better ways of quantifying ownership, but invites reflection on whether quantification risks reifying and operationalizing concerns that are themselves products of broader social, legal, and institutional arrangements. We therefore position this work not as a call to refine ownership-tracking systems, but as a critique of the impulse to computationally resolve questions that may resist technical solution altogether.

    4. (四)(d)

    您认为创作过程中的哪些步骤对所有权贡献最大？According to you, which steps in the creative process contribute the most to ownership?

  6. (6)

    你认为谁拥有最终的艺术品（你自己作为创作者/人工智能/人工智能的开发者/共享所有权模型）？为什么？Who do you think owns the final artwork (yourself as the creator/ the AI/ the AI’s developers/ shared ownership model)? Why?
