摘要Abstract

大型语言模型 (LLM) 正在改变数据库交互范例，从简单的查询翻译器发展为自主数据库管理员 (DBA)。然而，当前的评估基准仍然不成比例地关注文本到 SQL 任务，忽视了整体性Large Language Models (LLMs) are transforming database interaction paradigms, evolving from simple query translators to autonomous database administrators (DBAs). However, current evaluation benchmarks remain disproportionately fixated on Text-to-SQL tasks, neglecting the holistic数据库生命周期Database Lifecycle-从最初的架构设计到部署后维护。这种狭隘的关注点无法捕捉现实世界数据库管理所需的各种功能。为了弥补这一差距，我们引入-from initial schema design to post-deployment maintenance. This narrow focus fails to capture the diverse capabilities required for real-world database management. To bridge this gap, we introduceDBLifeBench，第一个评估法学硕士跨越五个关键生命周期阶段的基准：设计、实施、操作、调试和维护。 此外，为了解决模糊自然语言和复杂 SQL 逻辑之间的认知不匹配问题，我们提出, the first benchmark to evaluate LLMs across five critical lifecycle phases: Design, Implementation, Operation, Debugging, and Maintenance. Furthermore, addressing the cognitive mismatch between ambiguous natural language and complex SQL logic, we propose渐进式Text2SQLProgressive-Text2SQL，一项利用结构化推理图来模仿人类迭代问题解决的新颖任务。我们的广泛评估揭示了一个重要的见解：虽然通用模型表现出平衡的性能，但专用的文本到 SQL 模型在设计和维护等非编码阶段遭受“灾难性遗忘”。 DBLifeBench 是评估和构建真正的全栈数据库智能的基础步骤。, a novel task utilizing structured reasoning graphs to mimic human iterative problem-solving. Our extensive evaluation reveals a critical insight: while general-purpose models demonstrate balanced performance, specialized Text-to-SQL models suffer from “catastrophic forgetting” in non-coding phases like design and maintenance. DBLifeBench serves as a foundational step toward evaluating and building true full-stack database intelligence.

DBLifeBench provides the community with a rigorous standard to guide the development of the next generation of LLMs-true Autonomous Database Agents.

局限性Limitations

DBLifeBench 虽然很全面，但也有一些局限性。它主要是为法学硕士设计的，可能无法完全体现非法学硕士方法的表现。此外，某些任务，特别是设计和维护方面的任务，可能难以准确定义和评估。最后，当前的基准测试仅关注文本模式，不涉及多模式场景，例如解释视觉性能图表。未来的工作将旨在纳入多模式输入以扩大评估范围。DBLifeBench, while comprehensive, has some limitations. It is primarily designed for LLMs, which may not fully capture the performance of non-LLM approaches. Additionally, some tasks, particularly in design and maintenance, may be challenging to define and evaluate accurately. Lastly, the current benchmark focuses exclusively on textual modalities and does not address multimodal scenarios, such as interpreting visual performance charts. Future work will aim to incorporate multimodal inputs to broaden the scope of evaluation.
