DistP : Scaling Local-Update Training with Zero-Shot Hyperparameter Transfer

Andrej Jovanovi´c1,2 , Alex Iacob1,2 , Philipp Wiesner1,2 , Christophe Roux3,4 , Max Zimmer3 , Lorenzo Sani1,2,5 , Sebastian Pokutta3 , Nicholas D. Lane1,2,5**

1University of Cambridge 2Science of Fundamental AI Research Lab (SOFAIR) 3Zuse Institute Berlin (ZIB) 4CNRS - ENS, PSL Research University, INRIA, Paris, France 5Flower Labs

Abstract. Local-update methods offer a communication-efficient alternative to Distributed Data Parallelism ( DDP ), but introduce additional hyperparameters that require costly tuning. For DDP , zero-shot hyperparameter transfer reduces this cost by reusing settings tuned on smaller models, but its applicability to local-update training remains unclear. We propose DistP , a theoretical framework extending existing transfer rules from DDP to local-update training with varying worker counts, local steps, and outer optimizers. With averaging as the outer optimizer, DistP supports learning-rate transfer across width and depth while improving model quality. The tuned inner learning rate also directly transfers across synchronization intervals, but follows two regimes when increasing the global batch size: square-root learning-rate scaling for larger local batches and a fixed reference rate for additional workers. We then generalize width and depth transfer to other outer optimizers, enabling stable scaling with Adam . However, with momentum in the outer optimizer, changing batch size or local steps can require inner–outer learning-rate tuning, consistent with prior work. In language-modeling experiments at 823M parameters, DistP reduces validation loss by 8 . 1% and improves mean downstream accuracy by 10% relative to baselines without retuning at the larger scale. Fitted scaling laws suggest that this advantage grows with the compute budget, reinforcing the benefits of DistP for communication-efficient training.

1 Introduction

Efficient training of foundation models requires distributing computation across many accelerators [1]. In standard Distributed Data Parallelism ( DDP ), gradients are synchronized before every optimization step, creating a bottleneck on low-bandwidth networks. The community has leveraged local-update training to reduce this overhead by allowing workers to train model copies independently for several steps before combining their updates [2–4]. At synchronization, a shared model update is obtained by averaging these updates [5, 6] or by applying an outer optimizer step [7, 8].

Although these local-update methods can match the training performance of DDP while reducing wall-clock time in low-bandwidth environments [3, 9], they expand the hyperparameter search space and increase the cost of tuning. Beyond the learning rate and batch size, practitioners must choose the worker count, the number of local steps, and the outer optimizer and its hyperparameters. Moreover, the best settings depend on the training configuration, as changing the worker count or synchronization interval can shift the optimal hyperparameters [9]. This leads to configuration-specific tuning for these regimes, where the cost grows with model size [10].

A potential solution lies with zero-shot hyperparameter transfer, which enables the reuse of settings tuned on a small, inexpensive reference configuration at larger scales, reducing retuning. Existing work on this topic establishes the transfer rules, albeit for DDP : µ P [11] addresses width, CompleteP [12] extends transfer to depth, and Complete(d) P [13] incorporates batch size and token budget. These parameterizations also control how learned features change with scale, supporting better model quality. However, given that local updates introduce new optimization dynamics, the extension of zero-shot hyperparameter transfer to these regimes remains unclear. As such, we ask:

How can zero-shot hyperparameter transfer be extended to local-update training?

We introduce DistP , a scaling framework for local-update training. We establish theoretical conditions under which width and depth scaling applied on each worker is preserved under averaging and linear outer optimizers, such as Nesterov momentum, which form weighted sums of current and past updates. For adaptive outer optimizers, such as Adam , which normalize updates using running moment estimates, we derive additional scaling corrections. We then show why larger local

Hyperparameter transfer frameworks. Zero-shot hyperparameter transfer aims to reuse settings tuned on a small reference configuration at larger scales without retuning. In DDP , transfer rules specify how to adjust weight initialization, rescale layer outputs in the forward pass, and change optimizer hyperparameters to account for differences in the training configuration. µ P [11] provides these rules for model width, CompleteP [12] extends them to depth, and Complete(d) P [13] incorporates batch size and token budget. We refer readers to the cited works for the full prescriptions. Our central question is how these frameworks extend to local-update training, which we discuss in Section 3.

3 DistP : A Scaling Framework for Distributed Training

DistP , as illustrated in Figure 1, enables zero-shot hyperparameter transfer in local-update training (Table 1). Under suitable conditions, model averaging preserves width and depth scaling [11, 12] without further corrections (Section 3.1). More generally, linear outer optimizers preserve the same scaling, while adaptive outer optimizers require learning-rate and epsilon corrections (Section 3.2). In local-update training, the global batch size can increase by increasing each worker’s local batch size or adding workers. We show that, under averaging, the batch-scaling argument of Mlodozeniec et al. [13] extends to larger local batches, but not to additional workers (Section 3.3). Finally, we motivate local learning-rate transfer across synchronization intervals under averaging, at fixed global batch and token budget (Section 3.4).

3.3 Transfer across batch sizes

In local-update training, the global batch is computed as B = Mb , where M is the worker count and b the local batch size [9]. At fixed K , B can increase through either larger local batches b at fixed worker count M or more workers at fixed local batch size. Although prescriptions such as Complete(d) P express batch scaling in terms of B [13], these regimes affect optimization differently: larger local batches change the gradients entering each worker’s adaptive optimizer, whereas additional workers increase the number of updates combined by the outer optimizer. We analyze both regimes at fixed token budget D , so each worker takes T inner = MbDsteps.

Larger local batches, fixed workers. Increasing the local batch size has two effects: each worker receives less noisy gradients, but takes fewer optimization steps at a fixed token budget. 3.4 Transfer across local steps

At fixed global batch B and token budget D , changing K leaves the total number of local steps T inner = D/B unchanged. Increasing K therefore groups more steps into each communication round while reducing the number of rounds, R comm = T inner /K . Under averaging, we retain the reference learning rate as K changes, preserving this total step-size sum. Longer intervals can increase divergence between workers’ parameters and Adam moments, which is not addressed by this argument. We leave characterizing learning-rate transfer when K greatly exceeds the inner momentum half-life to future work. With outer momentum, changing K alters its update frequency and memory in local steps. These changes motivate joint inner–outer learning-rate tuning [14], as explored in Section 4.4.

4.4 Outer optimizers preserve model-size transfer (RQ3)

Following Section 4.3, we turn to examine DistP ’s generalization beyond averaging. Without additional corrections to outer Nesterov (Section 3.2), DistP preserves width and depth transfer and achieves lower validation loss than SP (Figs. 4 and 9). Like Charles et al. [9], we also find that the outer learning rate remains effective across model sizes (Section B.1). Notably, we find that DistP ’s learning-rate and epsilon corrections (Section 3.2) enable stable width and depth transfer with outer Adam , achieving lower loss than SP . This shows that Adam can be an effective outer optimizer, which Douillard et al. [2] found less effective than Nesterov . Next, we examine batch scaling with Nesterov as the outer optimizer. The Adam SDE’s square-root rule [13, 20] is an effective starting point for larger local batches (Figs. 5 and 12). Outer-rate tuning can narrow the gap to an unchanged inner rate (Section B.5). Joint tuning also reduces loss at some synchronization intervals, consistent with Khaled et al. [14]. Joint inner–outer transfer rules are left for future work.

5 Related Work

Hyperparameter transfer. Existing parameterizations reduce retuning by specifying how training settings change with scale. µ P [11] provides width transfer, CompleteP [12] extends it to depth, and Complete(d) P [13] incorporates batch size and token budget. Charles et al. [9] address hyperparameter transfer in DiLoCo by fitting learning rates as a function of model size, validating their prescriptions at 4B and 10B parameters. DistP addresses the same tuning problem by extending existing zero-shot hyperparameter transfer rules to local-update training, enabling transfer from a small reference configuration without fitting a new model-size law.

Communication-efficient training. Local SGD [6] and FedAvg [5] reduce communication by allowing workers to optimize locally before averaging parameters, an approach also adopted for language-model training in DiLoCo [2] and Photon [29]. SlowMo [7] and FedOpt [8] incorporate momentum or adaptive outer updates, making the interaction between inner and outer optimization central to hyperparameter selection. For quadratic objectives, Charles and Koneˇcný

9

[30] show that the inner learning rate changes the effective objective, while Khaled et al. [14] establish how the outer rate balances optimization error and gradient noise. Beyond learning rates, inner preconditioning also affects replica scaling [31]. Building on these findings, DistP extends hyperparameter transfer to local-update training by accounting for both inner and outer optimization. We provide an extended discussion of related work in Section F.

6 Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce DistP , which extends zero-shot hyperparameter transfer to local-update training by applying existing transfer rules on every worker and accounting for the outer optimizer. Our experiments show improved model quality, relative to SP , at matched compute and effective transfer from small to large models. This provides a principled way to scale local-update training from a tuned reference configuration without fitting a new learning-rate law across model sizes. Although width and depth transfer extend to Nesterov and, with corrections, outer Adam , batch and synchronization transfer with Nesterov can still benefit from further tuning. For larger local batches, square-root inner learning-rate scaling provides an effective starting point. Averaging, however, achieves competitive quality with simpler transfer and no outer learning rate to tune, making it a more effective and scalable choice for local-update training. We invite further theoretical work on joint transfer rules for inner–outer optimizer combinations to avoid repeated retuning.

F Extended Related Work

Communication and execution. Infrequent synchronization can still incur high communication volume and blocking. Streaming DiLoCo [37] overlaps communication with training, while SparseLoCo [38] compresses updates. Asynchronous DiLoCo [39] addresses worker heterogeneity, and Decoupled DiLoCo [40] tolerates delays and failures. AsyncMesh [41] corrects for staleness, while Factored Gossip DiLoCo [42] reduces blocking through approximate synchronization. These methods are orthogonal to our paper as they address the cost and scheduling of synchronization, where we consider how hyperparameters should transfer when model size and training configuration change.

23

Optimizer state and memory. Local training must also balance shared optimizer history against communication and memory costs. DiLoCo [2] retains local states, whereas Photon [29] resets them with intermittent clients. However, resetting Adam ’s second moment harms translation performance in SlowMo [7]. DES-LOC [15] reduces communication by synchronizing parameters and moments independently. MT-DAO [3] uses fast and slow momenta to retain shared information during local training. LoRDO [4] reduces optimizer-state memory through low-rank updates, while FoMoE [43] distributes experts across workers. These choices determine how optimizer history is retained and distributed. We study hyperparameter transfer under a specified optimizer and synchronization policy.

Batch and optimizer scaling. Batch-size gains eventually saturate [44, 45]. The Adam SDE [20] and Complete(d) P [13] motivate adjusting training settings with batch size. We examine how this extends to local workers. Scaling also depends on the optimizer. Matrix-preconditioned methods such as Muon [46] require appropriate model-size scaling [47]. MuLoCo [10] applies Muon locally, and Belilovsky et al. [31] link inner preconditioning to replica scaling. Our question is how the inner rule and outer update jointly determine transfer in local-update training. We assess this across training budgets and model scales [34, 48].

G Limitations

In our experiments, we focus on the use of inner Adam given that the zero-shot hyperparameter transfer frameworks were developed for this setting. However, we have not evaluated transfer with other inner–outer optimizer combinations. Further to this, we also assume synchronous updates, leaving open how asynchrony and update staleness interact with the transfer rules and optimizer-state synchronization. Finally, although our formulation includes token-budget scaling, our batch sweeps hold tokens fixed, so we leave its empirical evaluation to future work.
